# geom0006: refining the published box

**Question.** The box model of geom0005 trails a turn, because the course it is steered by is the
Kalman velocity's direction, which lags; it leaves a slow, square-on vehicle unlabelled; and its
rectangle fit is its whole cost. Does leading the course by its own rate, or weighing a turning
course less, cut the lag? Does the track's own displacement label a creeping car? Is the
minimum-area rectangle's axis a cheaper substitute for the fit?

**Rule, written before any replay (2026-10-17).** On the tuning set (the first segment of 23
sites and the two kirk-labelled captures), pooled over vehicle frames, a candidate passes when:

1. its target improves: the median magnitude of the turn lag (against a course centred ±3 frames)
   falls by 1.0° or more for a course option; the share of slow frames drawn across the vehicle
   falls by a fifth and by 0.2 points or more for a displacement label; the cost per update falls
   by 30 % or more for an axis source;
2. nothing else gets worse: visible turn-rate violations, turning at rest and visible jumps each
   no more than the box model's × 1.1 + 0.1 points; the straight-road error's median and p90 no
   more than 0.5° worse; no return outside; on kirk0's reviewed poses the axis p90 no more than
   1° worse and no more keyframes over 45°.

Candidates that pass are combined and scored on the tuning set, then on the fourth segment of
every site that has one, read for the first time. A candidate that meets both rules again there,
with at least half its tuning improvement, becomes the default; one that does not stays an
option.

**Round 1 (2026-10-17, before any held-out read).** No candidate passed. Leading the course by
0.4 s cut the median turn lag from 3.8° to 3.5°, short of the 1° target; weighing a turning course
less changed nothing; displacement labels left the slow-frame label errors where they were; the
minimum-area axis was cheaper but worse on the straight road (p90 8.2° against 6.6°) and at rest.

**Round 2 rule, written before its replays.** Displacement labels cut turning at rest from 3.6 %
to 2.1 % in round 1, a measure they were not chosen for. Candidates for that target: displacement
labels at 1 m, the unlabelled yaw-rate floor at 20°/s rather than 45°/s, and both. A candidate
passes when, on the tuning set, turning at rest falls by a fifth and by 0.5 points or more and the
round-1 non-inferiority rule holds, and then on the fourth segments it falls by at least half as
much with the same rule.

**Round 2 (2026-10-17).** On the tuning set the 1 m displacement label passed (turning at rest
3.64 % to 2.14 %, nothing else worse) and the 20°/s floor did not (turning at rest 4.02 %, kirk0's
reference p90 10.0° against 8.4°). On the fourth segment of 20 sites, read for the first time (two
more are shorter than the warm-up), the label took turning at rest from 2.63 % to 2.18 %, 0.45
points against the 0.75 the rule required. Nothing from this experiment enters the defaults; the
box model of geom0005 does, as its own result.

**Under the L3 retune.** The box model scored on the same tuning set under
l3-retune-cm2-mp8-floor.json: every one of kirk0's 44 reviewed keyframes matched, its axis 1.3°
from them at the median and 3.5° at p90 with none over 10°, and turning at rest 0.7 % of rest
pairs. Report: [published box refinement](../../../docs/lidar/operations/published-box-refinement-2026-10.md).
