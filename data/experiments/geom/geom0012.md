# geom0012: three centres a frame

**Question.** A frame gives the tracker three places to call the body's centre, and the
published scene draws two of them: the trail at the tracked position, which is the Kalman
filter's estimate of the cluster medoid (production since D5; E1 measured the medoid 0.35 to
0.65 of a half-width toward the seen faces at broadside), and the box at the box model's centre,
which the containment interval and the rectangle axis place from the returns. On the published
Columbus–Broadway scene (build 0.5.1-pre45) the two differ on the longest tracks by a median
0.4 to 1.7 m and by up to 5.9 m, read from the scene's own frames. The third, the cluster's OBB
centre, is D2's rejected candidate. Which centre is the body's, how far apart are they and
under what view, and which is steadiest frame to frame? The answer decides what the trail is a
trail of.

**Measures.** Per confirmed track and pooled per site, from one replay per arm:

1. _Offsets._ The tracked position less the published box centre, and the medoid less the OBB
   centre, signed along and across the course, by E1's aspect bins (end-on to broadside), by
   range and by class. The across offset at broadside is E1's bias re-measured against the box
   rather than a fitted path.
2. _Steadiness._ Each centre's frame-to-frame change, as an RMS per track on moving tracks, and
   the drawn box's centre slip from `lidar-box-eval`: which of the three moves least between
   frames while nothing physical moves.
3. _Truth, on kirk0._ Each centre against the 22 reviewed centres: the point-estimate arm and the
   solid-body arm through the physical scorer, the drawn box through `lidar-box-eval`; median
   and p90, cars apart from the trucks, as the W0 record asks.

**Reading, written before the replays (2026-10-18).** A measurement with one decision in it:
the trail draws the centre that is closest to the reviewed centres on kirk0 at the median and
no less steady than the tracked position. The prediction is that the two clauses split: the box
centre nearer the references (the box refinement report put the reviewed centre error at 0.66 m
with one track per vehicle, where the pilot's tracked position missed the trucks by 2.2 m) and
the tracked position steadier, because it is filtered. If they split, the trail draws the box
centre and the report states its RMS beside it: a trail is a claim about where the body was,
and a smoother line through the wrong place is the thing the research-preview notice on every
survey apologises for. The offset by aspect is reported whatever is decided; where its across
component at broadside is inside 0.2 of a half-width, E1's bias has been absorbed by the box and
the medoid state is a fair trail. `obb-centre` is read for one thing: whether D2's identity cost
(128 switches against 92) still buys a centre no nearer the references than the box's, in which
case it stays off for good.

**What could mislead.** The three centres share the cluster, so a merge or a fragment moves all
three and is not an offset between them; rows the tracker flags as merges are reported apart.
The box centre is held when its returns vanish and the tracked position coasts, so a coasting
frame's offset is two predictions, not two measurements, and is excluded. kirk0's references are
22 poses of three vehicles in one lane; the trucks are short in every arm for reasons upstream
of the centre (W3), so the car's rows decide the clause and the trucks' are listed.

**What it does not establish.** Anything held out. The vertical: the trail's z is ground0005's.
Which centre the following gap should read, which is l8fol0003's question, though its answer
is the same box.
