# l3bg0008: dwell policy, and the arrival regime on a long capture

**Question.** Every arm that recovers road users makes more long stationary tracks (on the corpus,
56 to 89 over 20 s). Does a dwell policy return them to the background without losing the
recovered road users, and how does the arrival regime grow with uptime on a 39-minute capture?

**Why `reclaim: origin`.** The whole-placement replay reads eight segments in order and its
evidence grows past 10 GB; a second worker restarting it from the beginning wastes the first
worker's hour. Leave an orphan for the worker that started it.

**Answer (October 13, 2026).** Yes on this capture: with the dwell policy no track stays
stationary for over 60 s (CMR 14), stationary track-seconds fall from 3,818 to 625, and moving
tracks only from 1,045 to 1,003. Under CMR the foreground grows with uptime; under the shipped L3
it does not. It also absorbs people standing for 20 s:
[report](../../../docs/lidar/operations/l3-dwell-policy-arrival-2026-10.md).
