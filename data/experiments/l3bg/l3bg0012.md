# l3bg0012: the L3 candidates on the corpus, label-free

**Question.** CMR confirmed 62% more moving tracks than the shipped L3 on the corpus and doubled
the long stationary ones; the dwell policy removed the long stationary tracks on one 39-minute
placement. Across the 23 corpus sites, does CMR with the dwell policy keep CMR's moving tracks
and remove its stationary ones, and how often does an absorbed object come back as a new track?

**Method.** The first segment of the 23 tuning and screen sites (embarcadero-folsom held out), 200
s scored after a 70 s warm-up, as l3bg0006 ran. The arms are the shipped L3, CMR and CMR with the
dwell policy; a lever from l3bg0010 that passes its kirk criterion is added as an arm, with and
without the policy, once that experiment is answered. Every count is label-free.

**Success.** Per site and pooled: moving tracks within a few per cent of CMR's, stationary tracks
over 60 s at or below the shipped L3's, and a re-track rate after absorption that is stated
against its control. If the policy costs moving tracks at many sites, or most absorbed objects
come back, Stage B (l3bg0009) needs a different T first.

**Answer (October 16, 2026).** CMR's gain holds across the corpus: moving tracks 1,331 to 2,081,
up at 22 of 23 sites, road-surface clusters to zero. The dwell policy at 20 s keeps every moving
track (2,084) and takes stationary tracks over 60 s from 19 to 1, but absorbed objects are
re-tracked 52% of the time against 33% at the same points without it:
[report](../../../docs/lidar/operations/l3-candidates-corpus-dwell-2026-10.md).
