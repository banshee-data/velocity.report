# l3bg0015: the dwell policy with a 40 s and a 60 s clock

**Question.** With a 20 s clock the dwell policy removed CMR's long stationary tracks over the
Lombard–Laguna placement but absorbed 16 tracks classed pedestrian, and an absorbed object came
back as a new track nearby more often than chance (41 of 86 against 28). On kirk it cost no moving
road user, but no one there stands for 20 s. Does a longer clock keep the stationary tracks down
while absorbing fewer people and causing fewer re-tracks?

**Method.** CMR with the policy at 40 s and at 60 s over the whole placement, replayed as l3bg0008
replayed its arms, and screened the same way: stationary tracks by lifetime and against uptime,
absorptions by tracker class, and for each absorption whether a track starts within 2 m in the
next 60 s, against the same points on CMR without the policy. The 20 s arm and CMR alone are
l3bg0008's. Uptime bins after 32:50 hold the knock to the sensor
([l3bg0013](l3bg0013.md)) and are read apart.

**Success.** A T that holds stationary tracks over 60 s at or near zero before the knock while
absorbing materially fewer pedestrian-classed tracks and re-tracking less than 20 s does, which
Stage B then runs with; or the finding that the pedestrian absorptions do not fall with T, so the
policy needs a class-aware rule instead.
