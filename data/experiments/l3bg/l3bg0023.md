# l3bg0023: one L3 for every replay, without losing tracks

**Question.** Since January a scaled or realtime replay substituted three L3 values for the
tuning file's: closeness 2.0 against 3, safety margin 0.30 m against 0.15, neighbour confirmation
5 against 3. Analysis mode, live input and every offline experiment tool ran the file as written.
The substitution is removed with perf0002 (#782), so every replay now runs one L3, the tuning
file's, and the measured pipeline is the published one. perf0002 measured what that changes on
three sites: against the substituted L3 the defaults find about half the clusters a frame and
28 to 38 % fewer confirmed tracks (535→385, 1,158→774, 4,141→2,576), with shorter lives. Nobody
has measured which of the two is right, because neither has been scored against labels. Before
the scenes are republished under the defaults, does moving to them materially lose real tracks
or shorten real ones, and if it does, which of the three keys should the defaults carry?

**Arms.** The defaults; the substituted L3 whole (`replay-l3`); and its two halves, since the
three keys pull in opposite directions: `closeness-2` widens the foreground band on its own, and
`margin-neighbour` narrows it (a wider safety margin, more neighbours to confirm). If one half
explains the substituted L3's effect, the defaults need at most that half, which is the
simplification the question asks for.

**Measures.** Label-free, on 23 first segments: moving tracks, tracks lasting 2 s or more,
confirmed vehicle-sized tracks, track life p50 and p90, track length p50, and duplicate pairs per
hundred vehicle tracks (more foreground makes more duplicates as well as more tracks). Labelled,
on kirk0 and kirk1: HOTA, IDF1, recall by class, identity switches and fragmentation, which say
whether a track the defaults lose was a vehicle or noise. The two kirk captures are the only
labelled ones and both are one site; the corpus says how general a difference is.

**Definitions, fixed before the replays (2026-10-18).** The corpus evidence holds each track's
estimates on the frames it was observed and the cluster each was associated with, but no class and
no confirmation state, so the corpus measures are defined from those:

- A **track** is a track id with estimates; a **lasting** track spans 2 s or more from its first
  estimate to its last, which stands in for confirmed, as in the earlier corpus screens.
- A **vehicle-sized** track is a lasting track whose associated clusters' longer box side has a
  90th percentile of 3.0 m or more; a **moving** one also has a 90th percentile speed of 2 m/s or
  more. "Confirmed vehicle-sized tracks" in the rule are the lasting vehicle-sized tracks.
- **Track life** p50 and p90 are over the vehicle-sized tracks, and **track length** p50 is the
  path length along their estimates, over the moving vehicle-sized tracks.
- A **duplicate pair** is two vehicle-sized tracks with estimates in at least 5 common frames with
  their centres within 1.5 m of each other in each of them: a vehicle split into two tracks on
  separate clusters.

On kirk, HOTA and IDF1 are `lidar-ground-truth-eval perframe` against the reviewed pack and split
(each arm as B against the defaults as A), and vehicle recall is the zero-code arms report's
object-frame recall for cars and trucks (`score_arm.py`).

**Rule, written before the replays (2026-10-18).**

1. The defaults are **not materially worse** than the substituted L3 when, on kirk0 and kirk1,
   their HOTA and IDF1 are within 0.02 and vehicle recall within 3 points, and on the corpus,
   pooled, their confirmed vehicle-sized tracks are within 10 % and their track life p50 within
   10 % of the substituted L3's, with the comparison repeated per site and no site more than
   25 % apart. Then the defaults stay as they are, and the republish runs them.
2. If the defaults fail clause 1, the candidate for the defaults is the arm that passes it with
   the fewest changed keys: `closeness-2` or `margin-neighbour` before `replay-l3`. It must also
   not raise the duplicate-pair rate by more than 2 per hundred vehicle tracks, and it is read
   once on the second segment of 22 sites, where it must keep at least half of its gain over the
   defaults on each measure it was chosen for. A candidate that passes moves its keys into
   `tuning.defaults.json` in a PR that recaptures the perf baselines for the moved fingerprint.
3. If no arm passes clause 2, the defaults stay and the report says what the labels show the
   extra tracks to be.

**What could mislead.** The substituted L3 was never scored, so "fewer tracks" may be fewer
duplicates and less noise; the labels decide, and the duplicate count is in the rule for that
reason. Tracks lasting 2 s or more and confirmed vehicle-sized tracks are the measures the
following metric and the scene pages rest on; moving tracks counts pedestrians and noise too.
kirk0's labels were reviewed on tracks from the defaults' L3, which favours the defaults on the
identity measures; the report says by how much the matching changes between arms.

## Answer (2026-10-19)

The defaults lose about half the vehicle tracks the substituted L3 finds, on the corpus (1,270
against 2,399 lasting vehicle-sized tracks, life p50 4.1 s against 4.8) and on kirk0's labels
(HOTA 0.376 against 0.542, IDF1 0.350 against 0.656). Closeness 2 alone carries the detection but
fails kirk's identity measures, so the candidate is the substituted L3 whole; on 22 held-out
second segments it keeps its gain (2,495 against 1,281). The rule passes it. It also brings five
times the spurious clusters on kirk0, three to ten times the long stationary tracks and twice the
road-surface clusters, which the rule did not measure; l3bg0024 compared it with CMR and made it
the defaults, as the best L3 measured.
