# l4clus0008: two unexplained kirk0 figures from the November run

**Question.** Two figures in the
[noise and splits run](../../../docs/lidar/operations/pcl-noise-and-splits-2026-11.md) were left
unexplained. Removing the L4 input cap lifts W3 retention from 0.851 to 0.993 and raises
identity switches from 87 to 148: why do more returns cost identity? And the radius graph raises
50 m+ retention from 0.779 to 0.853 and 50 m+ fragmented instants from 8.9 % to 17.5 % at once:
does it split far objects, or does it reach far sparse instants DBSCAN's core rule called noise,
which then arrive split? Both are answerable from kirk0 with no new code, and both decide whether
a candidate is retired or reread.

**Arms.** `a0`; `nocap` (the 100,000-point set, l4clus0005's control); `nocap-tentative`, the
same with tentative priority, the option that stops a confirmed track whose object dropped out
taking a newborn's cluster outside its 99 % ellipse; `radius-graph`, l4clus0005's arm.

**Rule, written before any replay (2026-11-05).** A measurement with two readings.

1. **The cap and the tracker.** If `nocap-tentative` brings kirk0's identity switches to at most
   `a0`'s plus 10 with retention at least 0.99 and HOTA no lower than `a0`'s less 0.01, the cap is
   hiding an association sensitivity, not saving identity: tentative priority moves to the head of
   the identity queue as the option every cap change needs, and the cap's own question becomes the
   Pi's frame-time tail (perf0001). If switches stay over `a0`'s plus 10, the cost of more returns
   is in the tracker in a way tentative priority does not reach, and the per-object switch list
   names which objects switch; the report says whether they are the objects whose returns the cap
   removed.
2. **The radius graph at 50 m+.** Reading counts, not shares: if the number of fragmented 50 m+
   instants under `radius-graph` is no more than `a0`'s plus 10 % while the number of instants
   reaching a cluster rises, the doubling is a denominator effect and the review recommends the
   owner reread l4clus0005's 50 m+ figure as a count, with the 20 to 35 m bar reset against the
   current baseline. If the fragmented count itself rises by more than 10 %, the graph splits far
   objects and is retired as an arm.

Nothing here changes a default or a bar; a reset is the owner's.

**What could mislead.** kirk0 has few far objects, so the 50 m+ counts are small and the 10 %
margin may be one or two instants; the report gives the raw counts. The two trucks dominate the
switch list, and a switch on a truck fragment counts the same as one on a car.

**What it does not establish.** Anything on the corpus; Pi cost; whether an uncapped L4 is
affordable live.
