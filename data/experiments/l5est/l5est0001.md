# l5est0001: the trail's noise model

**Question.** The published trail is the Kalman posterior of a planar constant-velocity filter,
and it does not move like a vehicle. On the Columbus–Broadway scene (build 0.5.1-pre45), read from
the asset's own frames on 73 moving vehicle tracks, the trail departs from constant velocity by a
median 13.5 m/s² RMS frame to frame, its exported velocity differs from its own finite difference
by 2.0 m/s RMS at a median 5.1 m/s, and each position step misses the step the velocity predicts
by 0.52 m RMS per 0.2 s. A car does not accelerate at 13 m/s².

Two things make a trail like that, and this spec separates them from a third it does not touch:

1. _The process noise._ The shipped Q is diagonal: `process_noise_pos` 0.05 m²/s added straight to
   each position variance, and `process_noise_vel` 0.2 to each velocity variance, per second. The
   position term is a random walk the constant-velocity model does not claim, and against a
   measurement noise of 0.05 m² it lets the posterior follow every measurement. The coupled form
   (`CoupledProcessNoise`, gap K1) adds the position-velocity terms the white-noise-acceleration
   model has, and a smaller position term leaves the motion to the velocity.
2. _The measurement noise._ G-UNC-1's kirk0 evidence found the shipped R already inside its band,
   and the fit lowered it by 35–45 %: a calibrated R makes the posterior follow the measurement
   more closely, not less. The `adaptive` arm is here to show its effect on the trail, not to
   calibrate it; that is G-UNC-1's.
3. _The measurement point_, which this spec does not change. The medoid moves over the body as the
   visible faces change (E1), and no noise model filters a bias out. geom0012 decides which
   centre the trail follows; this spec decides how the filter follows it.

**Measures.** From one replay per arm on the first segment of the 23 sites, and kirk0:

1. _Steadiness of the trail_, on confirmed vehicle tracks at 3 m/s or more over 20 frames or more,
   per site and pooled: the RMS departure from constant velocity, the RMS difference between the
   exported velocity and the position's central difference, and the p90 lateral residual from
   the track's own line.
2. _What steadiness costs_: confirmed vehicle tracks, duplicate pairs per hundred vehicle tracks,
   the turn lag p90 (a filter that trusts its velocity more turns late), and on kirk0 the tracked
   position's median and p90 distance from the 22 reviewed centres, cars apart from the trucks.
3. _The births_: tracks born and confirmed per site. A tighter gate, from a smaller R or a smaller
   P, also governs acquisition; G-UNC-1's synthetic scenes found the zero-velocity initialiser
   fragments fast bodies under a small R.

**Rule, written before any replay (2026-10-18).** An arm passes when, against `a0`: the trail's
RMS departure from constant velocity falls by a third or more pooled and on at least 20 of the 23
sites; the velocity disagreement falls; confirmed vehicle tracks are at least `a0`'s × 0.98 and
duplicate pairs per hundred vehicle tracks no higher; the turn lag p90 rises by no more than 2°;
and on kirk0 the tracked position's median distance from the reviewed car centres rises by no
more than 0.05 m. Of the arms that pass, the one with the smallest departure is the candidate,
and a candidate changes no default by itself: the coupled form is a Go option and the position
term a tuning key, so adoption is a decision with the perf baselines recaptured in the same
change. If no arm passes on steadiness, the jitter is the measurement's, and the remedy is
geom0012's centre and the smoothed export, not the filter's noise. `adaptive` is read for one
thing: whether a calibrated-direction R moves the trail at all, beside the arms that change Q.

**What could mislead.** A trail can be steadier and further from the body: the clause on the
reviewed centres is there for that, and it reads kirk0's 22 poses, three vehicles in one lane.
The second difference on a 0.1 s step amplifies the measurement noise, so the departure is a
comparison between arms, not a physical acceleration; the report also gives it on the 0.2 s
stride the published scenes use. A merged or fragmented track moves all arms alike and is
excluded through the tracker's merge flag, as lidar-box-eval already does for the box.

**What it does not establish.** Which point the trail should follow (geom0012). Whether the
offline smoother should draw the published trail instead of the posterior (the smoothed-export
spec). R's calibration (G-UNC-1). Anything held out.
