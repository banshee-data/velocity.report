# l5est0002: the published trail as the whole-track smoother's estimate

**Question.** The trail the survey pages draw is the tracker's online posterior, and on the
published Columbus–Broadway scene (0.5.1-pre45, read from its own frames on 2026-10-18) it
jitters: moving vehicle tracks depart from constant velocity by 13.5 m/s² RMS frame to frame,
where a car does under 3; the exported velocity differs from the trail's own finite-difference
velocity by 2.0 m/s RMS at a median speed of 5.1 m/s; and each position step misses the
velocity's prediction by 0.52 m per 0.2 s. The filter is a planar constant-velocity Kalman
filter with a measurement noise of 0.05 against a process noise of 0.05 and 0.2, so its
posterior is nearly the measurement, and the measurement is the medoid of whatever the
foreground held that frame. A published scene is an offline replay, so it need not draw the
causal estimate at all: the fixed-assignment RTS smoother that already exists behind the
`fixed_lag_rts` replay experiment can revise every state with everything the tracker saw after
it, at no lag, from the filter record a replay now keeps beside its VRLOG
(`velocity scene export --smoothed-trail`, which writes the smoothed x, y, vx, vy in place of
the posterior and names the trail's estimator and stage in the header). Does the whole-track
smoother make the published trail a trail of a vehicle, without changing what the scene says
happened?

**Arms.** One replay of each site under the shipped defaults, exported twice from the same
recording: `a0`, the online posterior (`trail_estimator: cv_kf_v1/online`), and `smoothed`, the
whole-track arm of the smoother (`cv_kf_v1+rts_fixed_assignment_v1/final`). No tuning key
changes; the smoother runs after the tracker and inherits every association it made. The two
exports of a site must differ in nothing but x, y, vx and vy: z, h, bx, by, spd, hdg, class and
every identifier are the tracker's own in both, and the reader's paired check refuses a pair
that differs elsewhere.

**Measures.** From the exported frames at the published stride, which are what the site draws,
per moving vehicle track (at least 20 samples, mean exported speed 3 m/s or more, median h
1.2 m or more, as the 2026-10-18 read defined them) and pooled per site:

1. _Departure from constant velocity._ The second difference of the trail's position over
   frame triples, divided by the frame interval squared, as an RMS per track; the figure that
   is 13.5 m/s² today.
2. _Velocity against the trail._ The exported `vx`, `vy` against the finite-difference velocity
   of the trail's own positions, as an RMS over the track; and the step residual, each position
   step less the exported velocity times the interval, as an RMS per 0.2 s.
3. _Lateral residual._ Each position's distance from the track's own fitted straight line,
   p50 and p90 per track; this is where a smoother that flattened a real curve would show, so
   it is read beside the course-rate row below, not alone.
4. _Lag along the course._ The smoothed position less the online one, projected on the track's
   course, as a median and p90 per track: a smoother that led or trailed the online trail would
   put the box and the trail at different points of the body, since the box (bx, by) stays the
   online one.

Also reported: the course rate preserved, the smoothed track's peak 0.5 s course-rate change as
a share of the online track's (the criteria document's screen row, which the whole-track arm
fails on synthetic lane changes at 69 %); the header's smoothing counts (states final against
fixed_lag, track frames revised against left online, barriers, covariance fallbacks, revisions
without evidence); and the pairing: track identifiers, lifetimes and classes identical across
the two exports of every site.

**Rule, written before any export (2026-10-18).** The option ships, as the published trail,
when on every site departure from constant velocity (measure 1) falls by half or more on the
pooled moving vehicle tracks, and no track loses identity: the two exports of each site carry
the same track identifiers over the same frames with the same classes, which the pairing check
reports and the fixed assignment guarantees by construction. Measures 2 to 4 are reported, not
gated: the velocity-to-trail residual is expected to fall with the departure, since the RTS
state is self-consistent; the lateral residual is read with the course-rate row, and a site
where it falls while the course rate kept is under 85 % is flagged as flattening rather than
smoothing; the along-course lag is expected within 0.2 m at the median, and a larger one is
reported against the box offset geom0012 measures. If the rule passes, `SCENE_TRAIL=smoothed`
becomes the Makefile default for `scene-assets` and the scenes are republished with the next
batch; if any site fails it, the option stays off by default and the report says which sites and
why. The whole-track horizon is the arm because the export is offline; no shorter horizon is
tried here, since none costs less offline and the criteria document's selection rule is for a
live stage.

**What could mislead.** A smoother returns the most probable path under the filter's own model,
and at the shipped process noise that model finds a manoeuvre improbable, so a fall in measure 1
can be bought by flattening a real turn; the course-rate row is the guard, and the sites with
turning traffic (the intersections, not the straights) are where it is read. Fixed assignment
smooths a wrong association into the trajectory rather than repairing it: a merge or a fragment
moves the smoothed trail as it moved the online one, and the identity rows will show the same
switches in both arms. The measures are read at the export's stride, not every tracker frame.
A track released at the window cap, a barrier or a reference change is `fixed_lag` rather than
`final`; their share is reported from the header, and a site with many is not a clean whole-track
reading. Two consecutive replays on one server process can carry state from one to the next
(perf0002), which is why the set exports both variants from one recording rather than replaying
twice; the compare step is the check that it did.

**What it does not establish.** Accuracy: no reviewed reference is read here, so a smoother
that moves every state toward a biased medoid is still biased (E1's 0.35 to 0.65 half-width at
broadside); the measures are label-free steadiness and self-consistency, and the report must say
so. Which centre the trail should follow, which is geom0012's; the vertical, which is
ground0005's. Whether a fixed-lag stage should be promoted for live reports, which the criteria
document's selection rule decides on held-out identity and geometry. Anything about the
process noise: a smoother over a mis-specified model smooths the mis-specification, and the
noise model is l5est0001's question, the first spec of this area; its arms change what this smoother would
be given, so a passing smoothed trail is re-read under whatever noise that spec adopts.
