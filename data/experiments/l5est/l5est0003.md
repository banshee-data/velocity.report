# l5est0003: the filter's velocity on kirk0's trucks

**Question.** figure0002 found the filter's speed within its reference bound for kirk0's car and
0.77 and 0.86 m/s (about 12 %) low for its two trucks over spans where their positions moved at
the reference speed: the velocity lags an accelerating long body. The velocity process noise
(`process_noise_vel`, 0.2) sets how fast the filter lets its velocity change. Does doubling or
quadrupling it bring the trucks' speeds within their bounds, and what does it cost in identity and
in noise?

**Arms.** `a0`; `vel-x2` and `vel-x4`, the defaults with `process_noise_vel` 0.4 and 0.8.

**Rule, written before any replay (2026-11-05).** A screen on kirk0's three referenced vehicles;
it changes no default whatever it reads, because three vehicles of a tuning capture are the only
speed references the repository holds and a held-out read needs label0002's.

1. **Target.** An arm brings both trucks' mean filter speed over their spans (figure0002's "row
   speed", now the instant speed) within their reference bounds, and keeps the car within its.
2. **Non-inferiority.** Against `a0`, the paired bootstrap's 5th percentile of the HOTA delta is
   above −0.02 and of the IDF1 delta above −0.02 (the kirk0 anchor's intervals are ±0.06), the
   identity switches' 95th percentile is under +10, and the noise screen's brief tracks rise by no
   more than 3.
3. **What a pass proposes.** The smallest passing arm is proposed for a held-out read on
   label0002's references and the corpus's speed figures (figure0001); it is not adopted.

**What could mislead.** A filter that follows acceleration faster also follows the cluster centre's
own jitter: the speed's maximum, which the published percentiles read, could rise on every track,
not only the lagging ones. The report gives each arm's maximum-speed p85 and p98 on kirk0 beside
the rule. Three vehicles: one turning truck can decide the target.

**What it does not establish.** Any speed above kirk0's slow traffic; a population's percentiles;
anything held out.
