# l5est0004: a looser velocity process noise as an identity lever

**Question.** l5est0003 asked whether a looser velocity process noise ends the filter's lag on
kirk0's trucks; it did not, for truck 2. But doubling `process_noise_vel` (0.2 to 0.4) took kirk0's
identity switches from 90 to 43 and lifted HOTA by 0.009 and IDF1 by 0.019, both gains keeping
their sign over 2,000 bootstrap draws of the objects, with brief tracks level. That was found by
looking, on the tuning capture. Does it hold on the corpus, where the overlap end's gains held and
its costs appeared, and on the held-out window?

**Rule, written before any corpus or held-out read (2026-11-05).** `vel-x2` against `a0`, scored
by site with a worst leave-one-out line:

1. **Duplicates.** Pairs per hundred vehicle tracks fall at 15 or more of the 23 sites.
2. **Costs, l5id0012's bounds with the footprint match.** Lost real tracks at most 1 % pooled,
   shortened at most 2 %, clutter added at most 5 per hundred moving tracks, moving tracks within
   `a0`'s × 1.02 at 20 or more sites, and the matched pairs' p95 |Δp85| at most 1.0 m/s.
3. **Figures.** At the median site, |Δcount| at most 2 % and |Δp85| and |Δp98| at most 0.2 m/s
   (figure0001's reporting bounds).
4. **Held out.** On label0003's spans, covered road users no fewer than `a0`'s and lost vehicles
   no more.

A pass on 1 to 4 proposes `vel-x2` as a default to the owner, with Pi timing (perf) and the
per-frame comparison on label0001's masks still owed. Nothing changes a default here.

**What could mislead.** A filter whose velocity moves faster bridges a gap more easily, which is
fewer switches on one vehicle and wrong joins between two: the corpus has no labels to tell them
apart, and the held-out spans are the check. The first segments have been read by every
experiment; they are a screen.

**What it does not establish.** Physical speed accuracy, which l5est0003 and figure0002 read on
three vehicles; Pi cost.
