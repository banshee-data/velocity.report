# l5est0006: guarded two-point initialisation, against the defaults

**Question.** The November benchmark (l5id0019, on the open PR #994) found that
`two_point_velocity_init` raised the scored-window p98 speed by 5 to 18 m/s at every case: +7.00 m/s
on kirk0, +17.74 m/s on ashbury-downey's first segment. It also added 30 switches on kirk0, with
an interval reaching +116. Instrumented replays traced the fast rows to two causes. The first is
reflections: thin, sparse clusters on a real car's bearing, at k times its range, moving at k
times its speed. The shipped tracker never confirms them, because it cannot hold anything born
above about 14 m/s. The second is pairs whose reference point slides with the visible extent,
such as a fragment and then the whole body. Commit `0f0316b8` declines a pair seen in a confirmed
track's shadow, and widens the velocity covariance by the reference point's slack, declining when
the cap cannot represent it. Does the guarded option stop inventing speed, and does it keep the
identity and fast-vehicle behaviour it was built for?

**Arms.** Both from `config/tuning.defaults.json`, fingerprint `ab0c4035780d`.

- `a0`: the defaults.
- `two-point-guarded`: the replay experiment `two_point_velocity_init`, built at `0f0316b8` or
  later. From that commit the name runs the guarded option.

The benchmark's unguarded arm, at `fe6549b3`, is the reference for what the guards changed. Its
figures are quoted from the l5id0019 report and are not rerun.

**Cases.** kirk0 (`20251205T1417_80858-75c_kirkham-9th`), whole, labelled, tuning data. The first
five minutes of `20260901T1225_80858-0ac_ashbury-downey` (`banshee-data/sf-street-speeds`), cut
by l5id0019's pcapng window filter. The cut must hash to l5id0019's segment, `971e093e…`.

**Rule, written before any replay of the guarded option (2026-11-09).** The guarded arm passes
when all three hold.

1. **(a) Speed.** On the scored-window rows, `lidar_figure_sensitivity.py`'s `p98_change`
   (guarded minus `a0`) is at most +0.5 m/s on kirk0 and on the ashbury segment.
2. **(b) Identity.** On kirk0's paired bootstrap against `a0`, the 95th percentile of the change
   in switches is at most +10.
3. **(c) Fast vehicle.** `TestTwoPointVelocityInitHoldsAFastVehicleFromBirth` passes at the built
   commit: the noise-free 20 m/s synthetic vehicle is one track.

A pass says only that the option no longer has the speed defect on these two cases. It does not
show the option helps. No default changes. A failure names its clause, and the option stays off
pending a further fix.

**Reported, not decided.** Each case's rows over 15 m/s, per arm. The benchmark's falsifier for
any fix to the initial velocity was "the variant still writes rows over 15 m/s where `a0` writes
none". The change in p85 and the count of moving tracks. kirk0's HOTA, IDF1 and fragmentation
intervals. How many pairs initialised and how many declined, against the unguarded option's 122
initialisations on kirk0.

**What could mislead.** kirk0 is a tuning capture, and the guards were designed after reading it
and the ashbury segment. The rule tests the defect on the cases that found it, not on held-out
data. The reflection attribution comes from row geometry (bearing, range ratio, speed ratio), not
from a labelled reflection class: kirk0's labels mark the object as noise, not as a reflection.
The shadow test reads the published box, which for a near car can span tens of degrees, so it
also declines real pairs behind a nearer confirmed body. Those pairs take the shipped update, as
`a0` does.
