# l5id0008: offline fragment links on kirk

**Question.** With the lane map from lane0001 and the link truth from l5id0007, does a linker
join the fragments one occlusion made without joining two vehicles? Does the lane graph and
global assignment do better than a reproduction of the paper's method, and does the tracker's
own occlusion evidence add anything?

**Method.** Each arm replays kirk0 and kirk1's labelled window with the default tracker; the
linker runs offline on the run's tracks and writes links and chains beside them, never changing
a track. `a0` links nothing. The thresholds are set once, from l5id0007's gap distributions,
before any arm is scored; they are not searched on the scores.

**Rule, written before the replays (2026-10-21).** On kirk's tuning split, over vehicle links:

1. an arm passes when its link precision is at least 0.98 (wrong links, counted per l5id0007's
   definition, at most one in fifty accepted), its chains' HOTA and IDF1 are each no lower than
   `a0`'s less 0.005, and its total observed vehicle seconds equal `a0`'s exactly; and when it
   pays for itself (the benefit floor):
   - its link recall is at least 0.50 of l5id0007's true vehicle links, and
   - its identity switches, scoring each chain as one track, are at most `a0`'s less half its
     accepted true links, rounded up: each true link should remove the switch at the
     reacquisition it bridges, and one that does not has not reached the scorer;
2. among passing arms, `lane-assign` is preferred over `dong-like` when its link recall is
   higher by at least 0.05 at no lower precision; otherwise the simpler arm is carried forward;
3. `lane-assign-occluded` is carried forward over `lane-assign` only when it raises recall by at
   least 0.03 at no lower precision.

Fragmentations (MOT16 FM) are reported and are not a benefit measure: FM counts a reviewed object
going from tracked to untracked and back whatever the track's identity, so a link, which adds no
observation, should leave it where `a0` has it; a change is explained in the report. The 50 m zone
count error against kirk's reviewed objects is reported for every arm and rules nothing.

**Why these floors.** Dong et al. report recall 0.916 with thresholds chosen for best F1 on their
evaluation data. Here the gates are set once from l5id0007's distributions and not searched, and
a wrong link costs more than a missed one, so the floor is lower: half. Below half, most
occlusion fragments stay split, and persisting links (C12 and a migration) would not be worth its
cost. At 40 true links a recall of 0.50 is 20 accepted, whose 95% interval runs from about 0.34 to
0.66; the report gives it. No arm changes a default; whatever passes goes to l5id0010's held-out window in shadow.

**What could mislead.** kirk is the tuning split and the gates come from it; a pass here is a
reason to look at held-out data, not a result. If l5id0007 found fewer than 40 true links, this
spec is answered as unreadable on kirk and l5id0009 reads rules 1 and 2 on its graded links, where
it runs `dong-like` beside `carried`; rule 3 is then not read, and the occlusion option stays
out. Precision on few links has a wide interval: the report gives the counts and a Clopper–Pearson
95% interval beside every ratio. Below 50 accepted links, 0.98 admits no wrong link at all.
Links with an unknown end (a track matched to no reviewed object) count in neither precision nor
recall and are reported apart.

**What it does not establish.** Merges (l5id0009), held-out performance (l5id0010), or any live
behaviour.
