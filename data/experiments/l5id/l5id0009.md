# l5id0009: links through a merge

**Question.** kirk has no declared merge. At a placement that has one, does the carried linker
keep its precision when a link may cross from one segment to another, and does allowing
transitions find links that refusing them would miss?

**Method.** A second placement, chosen by its geometry before a linker runs on it. Its map
declares the merge as a transition. Both arms run; a person grades every proposed link and marks
missed ones in the time-distance view without knowing which arm proposed it. Graded links are
the truth here, since the placement has no per-frame labels; they also serve l5id0008 if kirk
had too few.

**Rule, written before the replays (2026-10-21).**

1. `carried` passes when its graded link precision is at least 0.98 over at least 30 graded
   links, and at least 0.95 over the links that cross a transition, with their count reported;
2. transitions are kept in the linker when `carried` finds at least five more true cross-segment
   links than `no-transitions` at that precision; otherwise the first version refuses them and
   the report records the recall given up;
3. if l5id0008 was unreadable on kirk, its rules 1 and 2 are read here on the graded links, with
   `dong-like` as its baseline. Under 30 graded links the linker stops unshipped and the report
   says what truth would make it readable.

Every ratio is reported with its counts and a Clopper–Pearson 95% interval.

**What could mislead.** Grading without per-frame labels is a person's judgement from the
returns; where the grader cannot tell, the link is graded uncertain and counted in neither. One
placement says little about merges in general.

**What it does not establish.** Held-out performance; lane changes outside a declared transition,
which the first version does not model.
