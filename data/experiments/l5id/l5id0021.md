# l5id0021: a birth rule for the robust likelihood cost's fragments

**Question.** The robust likelihood cost (`robust_likelihood_cost`, #983) is the strongest identity
lever the November benchmark measured. On kirk0 it takes identity switches from 90 to 6, and
every paired interval against `a0` keeps its sign. It also raises brief tracks from 12 to 16 and
clutter from 15 to 19, over l5id0019's noise bound. This is in the benchmark report on the open PR
#994 (branch `claude/lucid-bardeen-ux375z-benchmark-nov8`, spec l5id0019), which ranks a birth
rule for these tracks second among the tracking fixes. Which clusters are these tracks born from?
Does a birth rule aimed at that population keep the identity gain and bring brief tracks and
clutter back within `a0`'s?

**What the diagnosis found, before this spec (2026-11-09).** A scratch tap replayed `a0` and
`robust` on kirk0 whole, with the same settings as l5id0019, and recorded every cluster, every
gated pairing and every track each frame. Nothing was scored against labels. Robust's 16 brief
tracks reproduce the benchmark's figure. 12 of them have a twin in `a0`, born within two frames
and 1.5 m of the same cluster. The other 4 are:

- **A static artefact `a0` hid.** A 0.44 by 0.28 m return at 11.7 m, born from the same cluster in
  both arms. Under `a0` its track coasted for 18 frames, then took a car's 1.47 by 1.07 m cluster
  from the car's live track and lasted 10 s. Under robust the car keeps its cluster and the
  artefact's track dies after 0.55 s. Robust is right here.
- **A real mover seen for one second.** A 3.9 by 0.75 m body, 90 points. Under `a0` an older
  coasting track took its clusters from the young track, and the young track never confirmed.
  Under robust the young track keeps its clusters and confirms. The body then leaves the
  foreground, so the track lasts 0.96 s. Robust is right here too.
- **A contest on one body.** A cluster split from a confirmed vehicle track's body seeded a
  tentative track in both arms. Under robust the newborn won the body's last two clusters, so
  the confirmed track's observed life was 0.87 s, not `a0`'s 1.07 s.
- **A fragment inside a coasting body.** A 0.27 by 0.19 m scrap lay 0.22 m from a confirmed
  2.45 m track's prediction. The fragment guard refuses the scrap to that track
  (`min_associable_extent_metres` 0.5). Under `a0` a duplicate track on the same body took it.
  Robust has no duplicate there, so the scrap seeded a track, which confirmed and died in 0.31 s.

Two broader birth rules were screened on the same taps and rejected. Barring births inside any
confirmed track's drawn box, grown by 0.25 m and one sigma, would remove 11 of robust's lasting
tracks. Among them are vehicles born inside a long-coasting track's box and two vehicles beside a
truck. Barring births from clusters another track's gate admitted would remove 3 lasting tracks,
two of them movers. One rule did fit. A fragment the guard refuses a confirmed metre-scale track,
lying inside that track's box, seeded 8 births under robust and 6 under `a0`. Robust confirmed 3
of the 8: two brief and one 3.4 s static artefact. `a0` confirmed 1 of the 6, a brief one. None
became a lasting moving track in either arm.

**The rule under test.** `robust_birth_gate` (`TrackerConfig.FragmentBirthGate`, commit `54dc1b7e`)
applies one test to each unassociated cluster. If some confirmed track's fragment guard refuses
the cluster, and the cluster lies inside that track's drawn box grown by 0.25 m plus the track's
position sigma (at most 1 m), the cluster seeds no track. It targets the fourth case and the
artefact beside it. It does not touch the first two cases, where robust is right. It does not
touch the contest either: that is an assignment between two existing tracks, not a birth. The
name is refused without `robust_likelihood_cost`.

**Arms.** `a0`, the defaults. `robust`. `robust-birth`, which is robust with the gate. `likelihood`
is plain `likelihood_cost`, the zero-code control the benchmark left open: it asks which half of
the robust cost acts. It is reported, and the rule does not read it.

**Rule, written before any scored replay (2026-11-09).** The rule is read on kirk0, a tuning
capture. Nothing is adopted and no default changes.

- **J1, identity kept.** The paired bootstrap of `robust-birth` against `a0` uses 2,000 draws of
  the scored objects, seed 20261108. The HOTA and IDF1 deltas must both have a 5th percentile
  above 0.
- **J2, noise back within `a0`'s.** This reads the evidence against `a0` with l5id0019's R4 bounds,
  which are what that benchmark counted as costing noise. Brief tracks may rise by 3 at most.
  Clutter may rise by 5 per hundred of `a0`'s moving tracks at most. Duplicate pairs per hundred
  vehicle tracks (`lidar-box-eval`) may not rise above `a0`'s. The strict comparison, brief and
  clutter no higher than `a0`'s, is reported beside it but does not decide.
- **J3, falsified.** The rule is falsified if `robust-birth`'s duplicate pairs per hundred vehicle
  tracks rise above `robust`'s: it would then remove the brief tracks but bring the duplicates
  back, which is the benchmark's falsifier. It is also falsified if its moving count against
  `robust` loses a lasting moving track by footprint (`lidar_flip_cost.py`).
- **Outcome.** `robust-birth` passes when J1 and J2 hold and J3 does not. A pass names it for the
  placements' label-free read and for label0003's held-out window, in robust's place. A fail
  names the clause and the population that remains.

**Expected, from the diagnosis.** Brief tracks 16 to 14 and clutter 19 to 16 or 17, both inside
J2. Switches and duplicates within 2 of robust's. The fragment that confirms under `a0` too
(seq 18 in the taps) is removed, so `robust-birth` may read one brief track fewer than this
reckoning. If the brief count does not fall at all, the gate is not reaching the population the
taps show.

**What was seen before the rule.** Besides the diagnosis above, the commit's wiring test ran
`robust` and `robust-birth` once on kirk0 without labels or scripts. It logged 81 against 78
confirmed tracks, 12 against 9 co-located confirmed pairs (a 2 m centre-distance proxy, not
J2's duplicate rule) and 43 absorbed births. No label, noise-screen or box-eval figure was read
for `robust-birth` before this spec was committed.

**What could mislead.** kirk0 is the capture the rule was shaped on: the population, the
one-metre sigma bound and the expectation all come from its taps. A pass here is in-sample, and it
is not evidence that the rule generalises. The placements and label0003 are where that is read.
kirk0 has 19 scored objects, and its HOTA interval half-width is 0.063 (the
[l5id README](README.md#the-kirk0-anchor)). Brief and clutter counts are tens, so J2's bounds are
three or four tracks. A pedestrian partly hidden by a vehicle can produce a scrap under 0.5 m
inside the vehicle's grown box. The gate would absorb it. kirk0's labels have no such case, so
J1 cannot see that cost.

**What it does not establish.** Anything off kirk0. Whether the contest case needs a rule of its
own: that would be an assignment rule, such as tentative priority under robust, not a birth rule.

**Answer (2026-11-09, kirk0).** The rule was not changed. `robust-birth` passes. J1: HOTA +0.0126
(5th percentile +0.0032) and IDF1 +0.0183 (+0.0055) against `a0`, with switches 90 to 6. J2:
brief tracks 14 against `a0`'s 12 and clutter 16 against 15, which is 2.6 per hundred moving
tracks; duplicate pairs are 8 against 10. J3 does not fire: duplicates equal robust's 8 pairs and
246 pair-frames, and no moving track is lost or shortened against robust. Against robust, the
gate removes exactly the three clutter tracks the diagnosis named and adds none. Identity does not
move against robust: no interval excludes zero. Strictly, two brief tracks remain above `a0`'s:
the one-second mover robust tracks correctly, and the contest. Plain `likelihood_cost` gives
nearly robust's identity (HOTA 0.4360, 6 switches) with 14 brief tracks. Nothing is adopted. See
the [report](../../../docs/lidar/operations/robust-birth-gate-kirk0-2026-11.md).
