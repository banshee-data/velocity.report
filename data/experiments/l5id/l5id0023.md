# l5id0023: the likelihood cost with the birth gate, off kirk0

**Question.** On kirk0 the likelihood cost takes identity switches from 90 to 6 without ending a
track, and the fragment birth gate holds its brief tracks and clutter within `a0`'s bounds
(l5id0021, in sample). Plain `likelihood_cost` scores as the Student-t `robust_likelihood_cost`
does there (HOTA 0.4360 against 0.4362, 6 switches each), so the term doing the work is `ln det S`
and not the heavy tail. Three things are unread: whether the gate holds noise on captures it was
not shaped on, whether the plain likelihood with the gate is the simpler candidate, and whether
tentative priority settles the contest the robust cost leaves. Then one arm goes to the first
truth off kirk0.

**Arms.** All on the defaults. `a0`; `robust-birth` as l5id0021 passed it; `likelihood-birth`,
the same gate under the plain likelihood; `robust-birth-tentative`, with `tentative_priority`.

**Cases.** kirk0 as the anchor. The first two five-minute segments of ashbury-downey and
haight-divisadero, which l5id0019 read for `robust` and which the gate has not seen. They are a
screen: the label-free held-out budget for identity and noise is spent on every corpus site
([November 5 review, F21](../../../docs/lidar/operations/experiment-review-2026-11-05.md#f-truth-held-out-budget-and-process)).
The held-out read is label0003's spans.

**Rule, written before any replay (2026-11-10).**

- **K1, the anchor.** Each candidate's paired kirk0 bootstrap against `a0` keeps a 5th percentile
  of the HOTA and IDF1 deltas above 0 (l5id0021's J1). An arm that fails K1 is dropped.
- **K2, noise on the placements.** On each of the four segments, under l5id0019's R4: brief
  tracks rise by 3 at most, clutter by 5 per hundred of `a0`'s moving tracks at most, and
  duplicate pairs per hundred vehicle tracks do not rise. An arm passes K2 when it costs no noise
  on at least three of the four segments and on no segment loses a lasting moving track by
  footprint (`lidar_flip_cost.py`).
- **K3, counts.** On each segment the moving count moves by 5 % at most and p85 by 0.5 m/s at
  most; a larger move is reported with the tracks that made it and fails the arm unless every
  one is a duplicate removed.
- **K4, which arm.** Of the arms passing K1 to K3, the one named is the simplest:
  `likelihood-birth` before `robust-birth` before `robust-birth-tentative`, unless a later arm
  has fewer pooled duplicate pair-frames by 20 % or more.
- **K5, held out, read once.** On label0003's spans the named arm passes when no span covered
  under `a0` is uncovered under it, the share of covered spans held by exactly one track over
  the span rises, and the track count over the window is no further from the span count than
  `a0`'s. A pass names the arm for label0001's masks and for the owner's default decision. A
  fail is recorded and the window is tuning for identity afterwards either way.

No default changes on this spec's answer.

**Reported, not decided.** Each arm's Tracker.Update and frame p99 against `a0`; the births the
gate absorbs per case; the pooled pair-frames; kirk0's switch and fragmentation intervals.

**What could mislead.** The four segments are quiet residential corners with 18 to 38 moving
tracks each, so K2's counts are tens and one track moves a clause. They carry no queues, which is
where an on-body rule fails. The gate's population, its one-metre sigma bound and its 2 m body
floor were shaped on kirk0. No capture read here has a pedestrian partly hidden by a vehicle, so
the gate's cost to one is unmeasured until label0001's masks. Span coverage counts vehicles, not
frames: it cannot see a switch inside a span that two tracks share in turn, which is why K5 reads
the one-track share.
