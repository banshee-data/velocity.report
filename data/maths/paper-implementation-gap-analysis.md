- **Scope:** Papers in [references.bib](references.bib) cross-referenced against production code (L3–L8). See [Source inventory](#source-inventory) for which papers were actually in hand for each section.
- **Method:** Trace each algorithm from the paper through the Go implementation. Note where the code deviates from the paper's intent, where edge cases go unhandled, and where the behaviour is plausible but untested.
- **Last revised:** 2026-09-19, against the paper texts themselves, the current code, and the results of the [2026-09 parameter experiment campaign](../../docs/lidar/operations/parameter-experiment-campaign-2026-09.md). See [Revision 2026-09-19](#revision-2026-09-19).

Of the 16 mathematical gaps, five are new in this revision (D6, K9, K10, B7, M5), one was reclassified upward (S3), and one is remedied in the replay path but open in production (G1). HW1's test is done; its remedy is not.

The three rows that matter most, in order, and why:

1. **S3** — association cost is bare Mahalanobis distance while coasting tracks have their covariance deliberately inflated, so a coasting track outbids a freshly updated one for the same cluster. DeepSORT names this exact failure and it applies here with more force than in the paper. Moving tracks coast on 34–57% of their frames in the measured corpus, so this is the common case rather than the edge case.
2. **M5** — the ground-truth evaluator matches whole tracks on temporal overlap alone. It cannot see state-estimate quality at all, and it scores a fragmented track as an undetected one. Every ground-truth result in the campaign is read through it. A per-frame replacement and its held-out harness now exist; the row stays open until the acceptance run on reviewed held-out episodes.
3. **B7 / HW1** — the L3 acceptance window is 86–98% model-imposed rather than learned, which is precisely what Stauffer & Grimson's per-distribution threshold exists to avoid. The campaign shows narrowing it has no settling cost.

## 1. DBSCAN: ester et al. (1996)

### Paper intent

DBSCAN defines three point categories: **core**, **border**, and **noise**. Border points are reachable from a core point but are not themselves core. The original paper assigns border points to the cluster of the _first_ core point that reaches them during expansion; a deliberate choice that makes the result order-dependent when a border point sits between two clusters.

Verified against the paper (now in hand), with three points the earlier pass did not have:

- **The neighbourhood includes the point itself.** Definition 1 is $N_{Eps}(p) = \{q \in D \mid dist(p, q) \le Eps\}$, and $p$ satisfies it. A core point therefore needs `MinPts` points _including itself_.
- **The parameters are global by construction**, and the paper relies on this when arguing that a point shared by two clusters must be a border point in both. Variable density is outside the algorithm's design, not an oversight in ours.
- **The paper supplies its own parameter heuristic** (§4.2): fix `MinPts` = 4 for two-dimensional data, plot every point's distance to its 4th-nearest neighbour in descending order, and take `Eps` at the first "valley". It is interactive and label-free.

### Implementation status

`l4perception/cluster.go` (`DBSCAN`, wrapped by `dbscan_clusterer.go`) uses 2D Euclidean distance in XY, grid-accelerated neighbourhood queries, and deterministic output sorting. When a frame exceeds `foreground_max_input_points` (shipped: 8000) it is uniformly subsampled first, with a seed hashed from the points so replay is reproducible. See [clustering-maths.md](clustering-maths.md) for the mathematical specification.

That is the direction D3's merge pathology predicts, and no value is a Pareto improvement at both sites, which is what a scene needing more than one density looks like (D5). It is not proof of either. The evaluator matches on temporal overlap only (M5), so it cannot tell a merged pair coming apart from one object breaking into pieces, and kirk1's +4 is inside that site's noise threshold of 5.

It needs no labels and no replay through the tracker. ## 2. Kalman filter: Kalman (1960), bewley (2016, SORT), weng (2020, AB3DMOT)

### Paper intent

- **Kalman (1960):** Optimal recursive estimation for a linear dynamic system by orthogonal projection. **Corrected against the paper:** the model is $x(t+1) = \Phi(t+1;t)\,x(t) + u(t)$ with $Q(t)$ the covariance of the random excitation, observed as $y(t) = M(t)\,x(t)$. There is **no separate additive measurement-noise term**. Observation noise is carried as extra state components ("signal plus noise"). The $R$ matrix, the discretised white-noise-acceleration $Q$ of K1, and the Joseph form of K2 are all later textbook developments (Bar-Shalom et al.), so this paper cannot validate any of them. What it does settle is the optimality claim: the estimate is optimal when the processes are Gaussian, and remains the best _linear_ estimate when they are not. Heavy-tailed cluster-centroid errors (see K9) therefore cost optimality, not validity.
- **Bewley (2016, SORT):** Kalman with a constant-velocity model on $[u, v, s, r, \dot u, \dot v, \dot s]$ plus one global Hungarian assignment for 2D MOT. IoU-based association cost with a minimum-IoU rejection, not Mahalanobis. Tracks are deleted after $T_{Lost}$ = 1 missed frame, in part because the authors consider the constant-velocity model a poor predictor of the true dynamics.
- **Weng (2020, AB3DMOT):** **Corrected against the paper.** SORT extended to 3D with an 11-dimensional state $(x, y, z, \theta, l, w, h, s, v_x, v_y, v_z)$ (angular velocity deliberately omitted) and association by **3D IoU** (or negative centre distance) through the Hungarian method, rejecting matches under $IoU_{min}$ (0.01 for cars). **There is no Mahalanobis gating in AB3DMOT**; an earlier revision of this doc said there was. Birth after $Bir_{min}$ = 3 matched frames, death after $Age_{max}$ = 2 misses. It also specifies an **orientation correction**: when the matched detection's heading differs from the track's by more than $\pi/2$, add $\pi$ to the track's, on the grounds that an object cannot reverse its orientation within one frame. See P3.

### Implementation status

`l5tracks/tracking_association.go`, `tracking_update.go`: 4-state CV Kalman `[x, y, vx, vy]`, 2D position-only measurement, Mahalanobis gating, Hungarian assignment. See [tracking-maths.md](tracking-maths.md) for the full specification.

### Measured outcome: K1 and K2

Both gaps are now implemented as opt-in `TrackerConfig` options — `CoupledProcessNoise` and `JosephCovarianceUpdate` — in [tracking_covariance.go](../../internal/lidar/l5tracks/tracking_covariance.go), with the tests the rows above asked for in [tracking_covariance_test.go](../../internal/lidar/l5tracks/tracking_covariance_test.go). They are Go-level options rather than tuning keys on purpose: `TuningConfig.Fingerprint` hashes the whole resolved config, so adding keys would make the committed perf baselines refuse to compare before anyone had measured whether the change helps.

**Both defaults are unchanged, because neither predicted failure mode reproduced at the shipped tuning.** The maths in both rows above is correct, and the alternative forms behave as the papers say. What the rows got wrong is the impact.

**K1 is real but immaterial at this tuning.** The coupled form adds the missing $dt^3/3$ and $dt^2/2$ terms, grows position uncertainty faster over a coast, and does reduce the normalised distance to a manoeuvring target. Measured against the test the row proposed — a target accelerating at 2 m/s² over one maximum-length prediction step (`MaxPredictDt` = 0.5 s):

| Form       | $d^2$ to the true position | $P_{x,x}$ | $P_{x,v_x}$ |
| ---------- | -------------------------- | --------- | ----------- |
| Diagonal Q | 0.2083                     | 0.25000   | 0.25000     |
| Coupled Q  | 0.2027                     | 0.25833   | 0.27500     |

A 2.7% reduction, against `GatingDistanceSquared` = 36 — a margin of **173×**. The row's proposed acceptance criterion ("verify the full-Q ellipse captures the target when diagonal does not") therefore cannot be met: the diagonal form captures it easily. Two reasons: `dt` is clamped to 0.5 s, which keeps the omitted $dt^3$ term small, and at these noise levels position uncertainty is dominated by the tuned `ProcessNoisePos`·$dt$ term anyway. The practical consequence is that **association at this tuning is governed by the width of the gate, not by the form of Q** — which is worth holding alongside K4, since the same wide gate is what makes identity mixing between adjacent unlike-class objects possible.

**K2's premise did not reproduce at all.** Over 10,000 predict-update cycles with adversarial measurement noise, the naive $(I-KH)P$ form's worst asymmetry was **exactly 0**, identical to the Joseph form, and the two settled to the same steady-state variance (0.005). Given an already-asymmetric $P$, both forms repair the asymmetry by the same amount — the Joseph form's advantage is that it cannot _introduce_ asymmetry, not that it repairs existing asymmetry better. The two agree element-wise to ~1e-8, i.e. to float32 rounding. So there is no measured instability here for the Joseph form to fix, which confirms this row's own "Low (covariance capping mitigates)" rating and makes switching the default unjustified churn against the perf and corpus baselines.

Both findings are asserted in the tests rather than merely logged, so that a future tuning change that _does_ make either gap bite — a tighter gate, a longer `MaxPredictDt`, or a conditioning change that starts losing symmetry — fails the suite instead of passing unnoticed. K3 (explicit $P = (P + P^T)/2$ enforcement) is correspondingly lower priority than its row suggests: there is currently no measured asymmetry for it to enforce away.

**Not yet corroborated by the campaign.** The "association is governed by the width of the gate" reading above was an inference from one synthetic target. The campaign's ground-truth sweep moved `gating_distance_squared` across 9, 16, 64 and 100 on two labelled captures and saw no visible change at either: labelled tracks recovered stayed at exactly 7 of 16 and 10 of 49, and candidate counts moved by at most 6. That metric was computed over rows written before #584, which stored each track as first sighted, so it could not see association over a track's life, and its indifference to the gate is weak evidence. K10 works out why the gate should not matter; a per-frame comparison (M5) is what would confirm it.

**One scalar is wrong in both directions at once.** Pooled over all speeds the mean NIS is 0.59–0.84: the filter is under-confident, dominated by slow and stationary tracks whose clusters barely move. For moving tracks it is 1.7–3.2 with two to three times the nominal exceedance: over-confident. No single value of `measurement_noise` fixes both.

**The error is along-track.** At the shipped tuning the steady-state innovation standard deviation the filter _expects_ is $\sqrt{S}$ = 0.28 m per axis (computed: prior $P_{xx}$ = 0.030, $R$ = 0.05). Lateral RMS is 0.21–0.39 m, which is that. Longitudinal RMS is 0.41–0.68 m, which is 1.5–2.4 times it, at every site and band. That is the signature of a centroid sliding along a vehicle as the visible surface changes, which is a measurement-model error and not something an isotropic $R$ can represent.

**These figures flatter the filter.** They are accepted-only, so censored by association, and the instrumentation's own notes point out that NIS also rises with motion-model mismatch, so a manoeuvring vehicle inflates it without $R$ being at fault.

**What this means for the campaign.** The preliminary L5 pass found that raising `measurement_noise` improved the alignment metric, and correctly refused to act on a metric that rewards smoothing. The third pass then concluded there is "no metric that can see" the L5 noise parameters and left them unswept. NIS can see them. It needs no labels; it is not circular in the RC6 sense, because it scores each raw measurement against a prediction made before the measurement was seen; and it penalises over-smoothing rather than rewarding it, since inflating $R$ drives NIS below 2. It is already instrumented and already persisted. A sweep of `process_noise_pos`, `process_noise_vel` and `measurement_noise` scored on per-band mean NIS and exceedance is runnable on the whole 24-site corpus today.

One erratum follows from the same maths. The L5 experiment's secondary check proposes that the innovation variance "should match the configured `measurement_noise` within a factor of 2". The innovation covariance is $S = HPH^T + R$, not $R$; it exceeds $R$ at every step. The check should compare against $S$, which is what NIS does.

## 4. Background model: Welford (1962), stauffer & grimson (1999)

### Paper intent

- **Welford (1962):** Numerically stable single-pass algorithm for running mean and variance: $M_n = M_{n-1} + (x_n - M_{n-1})/n$, $S_n = S_{n-1} + (x_n - M_{n-1})(x_n - M_n)$, variance $= S_n/(n-1)$. All samples weighted equally. Not in hand, and not used: see B6.
- **Stauffer & Grimson (1999):** GMM background subtraction with multiple modes per pixel. **Now verified against the paper**, which adds four specifics:
  - **The match window is learned, per distribution:** an observation matches a component when it lies within 2.5 standard deviations _of that component_. The authors make a point of this being a per-pixel, per-distribution threshold, and warn that a uniform one makes objects vanish where the local noise is lower than the threshold assumes.
  - **Update:** $\mu_t = (1-\rho)\mu_{t-1} + \rho X_t$ and $\sigma^2_t = (1-\rho)\sigma^2_{t-1} + \rho (X_t - \mu_t)^2$ with $\rho = \alpha\,\eta(X_t \mid \mu, \sigma)$: an EMA of _squared_ deviation about the _updated_ mean. $1/\alpha$ is stated to be the time constant.
  - **Unmatched observations never touch an existing component.** They replace the least probable one. So something absorbed into the background does not destroy the model it displaced: the old surface is still there, with lower weight, when the object leaves.
  - **Background is a ranked subset:** components ordered by $\omega/\sigma$, the first $B$ whose weights sum past a threshold $T$; $K$ is 3 to 5.

### Implementation status

`l3grid/background.go`, `foreground.go`: Single-component EMA per cell; not Welford's exact algorithm, not a multi-modal GMM. $\mu \leftarrow (1-\alpha)\mu + \alpha x$, spread: $(1-\alpha)s + \alpha |x - \mu_{old}|$. See [background-grid-settling-maths.md](background-grid-settling-maths.md) for the full model and [proposals/20260219-unify-l3-l4-settling.md](proposals/20260219-unify-l3-l4-settling.md) for the related settling proposal.

### Derived and measured: B2

The settling criterion requires the mean absolute frame-over-frame change in per-cell spread to fall to `settling_max_spread_delta` = 0.001 m. From the update equation $\Delta s = \alpha(\lvert d \rvert - s)$. For a settled cell with Gaussian deviations $d \sim N(0, \sigma^2)$, $s \to 0.798\sigma$ and

$$E\lvert\Delta s\rvert \approx \alpha \, E\big\lvert \lvert d\rvert - E\lvert d\rvert \big\rvert = 0.483\,\alpha\,\sigma$$

(the constant computed numerically and confirmed by simulation: at σ = 50 mm, α = 0.02 gives 0.000484 against a predicted 0.000483). **A perfectly settled grid has a spread-delta floor proportional to α**, so the criterion is a joint constraint on $\alpha\bar\sigma$, not a test of convergence alone:

| `background_update_fraction` | Time constant | Largest mean σ that can ever pass | Campaign: sites regressed (of 24) |
| ---------------------------- | ------------- | --------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| 0.005                        | 200 frames    | 414 mm                            | 0                                 |
| 0.01                         | 100 frames    | 207 mm                            | 0                                 |
| 0.02 (shipped)               | 50 frames     | 104 mm                            | baseline                          |
| 0.05                         | 20 frames     | 41 mm                             | 6                                 |
| 0.1                          | 10 frames     | 21 mm                             | 17                                |
| 0.2                          | 5 frames      | 10 mm                             | 24                                |

HW1 measured median spreads of 6–47 mm with a long tail, on a different deployment, so the comparison is order-of-magnitude only. But it puts the grid-mean σ in the tens of millimetres, which is exactly where the predicted ceiling crosses it and exactly where the campaign's regressions begin. The fifth pass's observation that at 0.2 the median site is settled for only about half the window after first converging is the same effect: the grid is hovering at its jitter floor, crossing the threshold in both directions.

Two consequences. The campaign's "sensitive" verdict on this key is at least partly a property of the criterion rather than of the background model: a faster-adapting grid is not necessarily a worse one, it is one the criterion cannot pass. And the criterion would be α-invariant if it compared $\lvert\Delta s\rvert/\alpha$, or the relative change $\lvert\Delta s\rvert/s$, against its threshold.

**The estimator is sound; its consumer was not.** Every degenerate input the P1 row asks about — five identical points, a vertical column sharing an X and Y, collinear points on four different axes, near-circular clusters at 150 m, a 1e6-magnitude centroid with a 1e-9 spread — produces finite, non-negative, correctly-bounding geometry with a deterministic heading. The 0×0 OBB is exactly as correct as the row says.

What the row did not look at is what happens next. `tracking_update.go`'s Guard 2 exists to refuse a heading from a near-square cluster, where the principal axis is ambiguous. It divided by the larger planar extent and so guarded that division with `if maxDim > 0`, which meant **a box with no extent at all skipped the ambiguity check entirely**. Measured against a track holding a heading 30° away (close enough that Guard 3's 60–120° axis-swap window stays out of it):

| Cluster              | Guard 2 result | Heading movement |
| -------------------- | -------------- | ---------------- |
| 0 × 0 m (degenerate) | accepted       | 0.042 rad        |
| 0.05 × 0.05 m        | locked         | 0                |
| 4.5 × 1.9 m (car)    | accepted       | 0.042 rad        |

The maximally ambiguous measurement was treated identically to a clean vehicle observation, while a 5 cm square was correctly refused. Guard 2 now reports a zero-extent box as `HeadingSourceInsufficient` — already defined as "missing or invalid geometry", and already counted as locked, so no lock accounting changes — which also keeps a degenerate cluster distinguishable from an ordinary near-square lock in a recording.

This is a latent defect rather than an observed one. Exact float equality of X and Y across `MinPointsForPCA` (4) returns is not something the sensor's own trigonometry produces from distinct beams; dual-return mode on a single ring and azimuth is the only plausible route, and no corpus occurrence has been identified. The full Go suite is unchanged by the fix, which is consistent with it never firing on the reference captures. It was worth fixing because the guard now says what it means and the case costs nothing to handle correctly.

**P2 is closed by removing the branch rather than correcting it.** The row is right that `lambda1 = c00` is the wrong eigenvalue when `c11 > c00`, and right that a real symmetric covariance cannot have a negative discriminant. The two facts together mean the branch could only be entered through float cancellation in `trace² - 4·det`, which requires `c00 ≈ c11` — precisely the condition under which `c00` happens to be approximately right. So the fallback was unreachable-in-effect rather than dangerous. It is now gone: the discriminant is computed as `(c00-c11)² + 4·c01²`, the algebraically identical sum-of-squares form, which cannot be negative and cannot cancel. The equivalence is pinned by a test, and the case the branch was meant to cover (perpendicular variance dominating) is tested directly.

## 6. SORT / deepSORT: bewley (2016), wojke (2017)

### Paper intent

Both corrected against the papers.

- **SORT:** Kalman filter (CV model on bounding box space [u, v, s, r, u̇, v̇, ṡ]) plus **one global Hungarian assignment over all existing targets**, with IoU distance as cost and a minimum-IoU rejection. New tracks serve a probationary period; tracks die after $T_{Lost}$ = 1 missed frame. **SORT does not process confirmed tracks first**; an earlier revision of S2 said it did. The authors observe that IoU cost handles short occlusions implicitly, because it favours detections of similar scale.
- **DeepSORT:** Adds an appearance descriptor, a Mahalanobis gate at the 95% $\chi^2$ quantile (9.4877 for its four-dimensional measurement), and a **matching cascade ordered by time since last update**, not by confirmed-versus-tentative. Tracks matched in the previous frame are assigned first, then those unmatched for one frame, and so on up to $A_{max}$ = 30; a final IoU stage handles unconfirmed tracks and those just missed. Tracks are tentative for their first three frames. The authors' stated reason for the cascade is the important part. When a track coasts, its predicted covariance grows, and they note that when two tracks compete for one detection the Mahalanobis distance "favors larger uncertainty": any detection is fewer standard deviations from the vaguer track. They name the result as fragmentation and unstable tracks.

### Implementation status

The tracker follows the SORT architecture but differs in three respects: world-frame position instead of image-frame bounding box, Mahalanobis cost instead of IoU, and no appearance features. All non-deleted tracks, tentative and confirmed, fresh and coasting, go into one cost matrix ([tracking_association.go](../../internal/lidar/l5tracks/tracking_association.go)). See [tracking-maths.md](tracking-maths.md) and [proposals/20260222-geometry-coherent-tracking.md](proposals/20260222-geometry-coherent-tracking.md).

### Gaps

### Computed: S3

From the shipped tuning at $dt$ = 0.1 s (diagonal $Q$, $R$ = 0.05, inflation 0.5 per miss). "Fresh" is a track at steady state that was updated last frame. Cost of a cluster 0.3 m from each track's prediction:

| ------------------------ | ----------------------- | -------- | --------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| 0 (fresh)                | 0.080 m²                | 1×       | 1.12                  | —                                            |
| 1                        | 0.595 m²                | 7.4×     | 0.15                  | 2.7× the distance                            |
| 3                        | 1.636 m²                | 20×      | 0.055                 | 4.5× the distance                            |
| 5                        | 2.694 m²                | 34×      | 0.033                 | 5.8× the distance                            |

A track that missed one frame outbids a track that was updated last frame, for the same cluster, from nearly three times as far away. These were computed from the configuration. **They are now measured as well:** the test drives the real `Update` path (two stationary pedestrian-sized objects 1.2 m apart tracked to steady state, one then unobserved for $n$ frames) and reproduces the ratios exactly: 7.4×, 20.3× and 33.5× at 1, 3 and 5 misses. A population of short-lived coasting tracks, each preferred by the cost over the established track next to it, would do that. So would other things, and the evaluator cannot separate them (M5). It is a reason to run the test above, not a substitute for it.

**`CascadedAssociation` does not address this.** The default-off option added on `dd/state-est/gap-fixes` orders by confirmation state: confirmed tracks choose first, tentative tracks take what is left. That is S2's remedy. Both S3 bidders are confirmed, so they meet in the same stage under the same cost, and the test pins that the outcome is unchanged with the option on. For the same reason the option cannot explain, or be evaluated against, the `hits_to_confirm` = 1 result above: at 1 every track is confirmed on its first hit and the cascade has one stage.

**Remedies, in order of fidelity to the literature.** DeepSORT's cascade: solve the assignment for tracks updated last frame first, then offer the remaining clusters to tracks one frame stale, and so on; this also resolves S2, since a tentative track is by construction recent. Alternatively keep one global assignment and make the cost a likelihood rather than a distance, $d^2 + \ln\lvert S\rvert$: the log-determinant charges a vague track for its vagueness (one missed frame costs $2\ln 7.4 \approx 4.0$, which reverses the 0.3 m example above: 1.12 against 4.15). The test checks the likelihood form's arithmetic against the real covariances: it reverses all four pinned cases, and it still returns a reappearing object to the coasting track that predicted it at 1, 3 and 5 misses. At one miss and an equidistant 0.6 m cluster it is nearly a tie (−0.56 against −0.43), which is the right answer: 0.6 m is a 2.1σ miss for the fresh track, so neither owner is clearly better, where the shipped cost prefers the coasting one by 7.4×. Either remedy is a behaviour change and needs the replay evidence and baseline recapture that implies.

## 7. MOT evaluation: bernardin (2008, CLEAR MOT), luiten (2021, HOTA), milan (2016, MOT16)

### Paper intent

- **CLEAR MOT (Bernardin 2008):** Defines MOTA (accuracy) and MOTP (precision). MOTA = 1 - (FN + FP + IDSW) / GT. Simple, widely used, prone to rewarding detectors over associators. Still not in hand, but MOT16 §4.1 and HOTA both restate the definitions and the matching procedure in full.
- **HOTA (Luiten 2021):** A higher-order metric that jointly evaluates detection and association quality, decomposed into DetA (detection accuracy) and AssA (association accuracy). **Matching is per frame and spatial:** a bijective Hungarian match between predicted and ground-truth detections in every frame, under a localisation similarity $S \in [0, 1]$. For point representations the paper gives one minus Euclidean distance, zero beyond one metre, which is the form that fits a centroid tracker. It also defines a fragmentation-aware variant (FA-HOTA).
- **MOT16 (Milan 2016):** Benchmark protocol. Two details bear directly on our evaluator. **Fragmentation (FM)** is defined exactly: a count of ground-truth trajectories going from tracked to untracked and back. And **ambiguous objects are neither rewarded nor penalised**: results are matched first, then any result matched to a distractor or ignore-class annotation is removed before scoring.

### Implementation status

`l8analytics/comparison.go` computes temporal IoU between track pairs across runs. `l8analytics/summary.go` computes aggregate run statistics. `adapters/ground_truth.go` (`EvaluateGroundTruth`, now reachable offline through [lidar-ground-truth-eval](../../cmd/tools/lidar-ground-truth-eval/main.go)) scores a candidate run against a human-labelled reference run on temporal overlap. Per-frame metrics now exist beside it: [perframe.go](../../internal/lidar/l8analytics/perframe.go) (MOTA, MOTP, identity switches, MOT16 fragmentation, the ignore rule), [hota.go](../../internal/lidar/l8analytics/hota.go) (HOTA, DetA, AssA) and [identity.go](../../internal/lidar/l8analytics/identity.go) (IDF1, IDP, IDR), under a fixed gate or the D2 A/B's footprint gate. [perframeeval](../../internal/lidar/perframeeval/doc.go) scores them against reviewed, held-out annotation episodes, and `lidar-ground-truth-eval perframe` fronts it: see [per-frame evaluation](../../docs/lidar/operations/per-frame-evaluation.md). See the [lidar-l8-analytics plan](../../docs/plans/lidar-l8-analytics-l9-endpoints-l10-clients-plan.md) for the planned analytics extension.

### Consequences: M5

Four, each visible in the campaign's results.

**It cannot see state estimates at all.** A track's start and end times do not move when its position or velocity estimates do. The campaign found this empirically (27 combinations of the three Kalman noise parameters gave 10 or 11 of 49 every time) and correctly concluded the grid had measured the metric. This is why K9's NIS matters: it is the available metric that does see them.

**It scores fragmentation as non-detection.** Matching is one-to-one on whole-track overlap. A reference track followed perfectly but in four equal pieces has a best temporal IoU of 0.25 with any of them, which is under the threshold, so it counts as _unmatched_; in three pieces it scrapes through at 0.33 and the other two pieces count as false positives. "Matched 7 of 16" therefore does not mean nine vehicles went unseen. With 81 candidate tracks in an 83-second capture, fragmentation is at least as likely a reading as non-detection, the evaluator cannot say which, and the pipeline's most important failure mode is invisible by construction: `Fragmentation` is the hard-coded zero.

**Its false-positive term penalises ambiguity.** Any candidate not matched to a positive-labelled reference counts against the score, including those that correspond to reference tracks nobody labelled: 77 of 141 in the richest reference run. MOT16's protocol is the opposite and is explicit about why. The campaign worked around this by never ranking on the composite score, which was the right call.

**The reference is not independent ground truth.** It is an earlier run of this same tracker with human class labels attached. Its track boundaries, against which temporal IoU is computed, are that run's own births, deaths and fragmentations. A candidate that tracks a vehicle in one piece where the reference run broke it in two is scored as _worse_.

The matching threshold is also stated inconsistently: the code uses IoU > 0.3, while the L5 experiment doc and the pipeline review's Q6 both specify ≥ 0.5.

None of this invalidates what the campaign concluded, because its analysis was built to be conservative about exactly these weaknesses (Pareto classes rather than rankings, noise thresholds from repeated baselines). It does bound what can be concluded next: more sweeps through this evaluator will keep measuring track _existence_. The fifth pass's label-free arm has the same property by design.

**Remedy status.** Each consequence above now has a counterpart in the per-frame harness ([per-frame evaluation](../../docs/lidar/operations/per-frame-evaluation.md)). Matching is per frame and spatial, so estimates are visible (MOTP, HOTA's localisation sweep). Fragmentation is MOT16's count, and one object in pieces scores as identity damage, not misses. Uncertifiable annotations are ignore points under MOT16's rule, so a hypothesis on an unlabelled or unreviewed object is neither rewarded nor penalised. The reference is a person's reviewed masks in an annotation pack, bound by digest to a frozen object-disjoint split. Two findings came out of building it. The ignore rule needed two refinements beyond MOT16's devkit: an ignored point may not keep a hypothesis by continuity, and a correspondence carries across frames in which its object is ignored. And the shared Hungarian solver was inexact for the matcher's frames with more references than hypotheses (H1). What stays open is the acceptance run itself: reviewed held-out episodes, and `final` estimates, which the pipeline does not yet write.

## 8. Patchwork++: lim et al. (2022)

### Paper intent

**Corrected against both papers.** The concentric zone model, per-zone plane fitting (R-GPF) and the ground likelihood test on uprightness, elevation and flatness are **Patchwork** (Lim 2021, now in hand), which Patchwork++ inherits. Patchwork++ adds four things: reflected-noise removal (RNR), which discards low-intensity virtual points below the ground in the bottom rings; region-wise _vertical_ plane fitting (R-VPF), which removes wall-like points from a bin before the ground fit so they cannot tilt it; adaptive likelihood thresholds (A-GLE) learned from previously accepted ground; and temporal ground revert (TGR). ## 10. Constant velocity model: schöller et al. (2020)

### Paper intent

**Rewritten against the paper; the earlier summary was wrong in both its claims.**

The paper shows that a constant velocity model (extrapolating a pedestrian's **most recent single step**, with no filtering at all) outperforms state-of-the-art neural predictors on the ETH/UCY pedestrian benchmarks, at the field's standard setting of 3.2 s observed and **4.8 s predicted**. Average displacement error 0.39 m, final displacement error 0.83 m. It attributes this to three things: learned environmental priors generalise badly, interactions are too complex to predict, and **motion history beyond the last step is not predictive**. It tests the last directly: a network given the full eight-step history scores an average displacement error of 0.42 m, and given a single step, 0.41 m.

It does **not** show that CV degrades beyond five seconds (it evaluates one horizon), does not mention process noise (there is no Kalman filter in it), and does not recommend tuning the observation window (it finds the window irrelevant). It concerns pedestrians only.

One further result matters here: the authors single out a **constant acceleration** baseline as the worst performer, at roughly double CV's error (0.84 m average, 2.11 m final). It is the worst of the ten models on final error; an LSTM baseline is worse on average error because of one scene. The authors' reading is that assuming continued acceleration is detrimental over long horizons.

The tracker uses a CV model. The paper supports that choice for pedestrians at horizons well beyond any this tracker predicts over: the longest coast is `max_misses_confirmed` = 15 frames, 1.5 s. See [tracking-maths.md](tracking-maths.md) for the noise parameter specification.

### Measured outcome: HW1

Tested in [background_closeness_test.go](../../internal/lidar/l3grid/background_closeness_test.go), against the threshold formula extracted into [background_closeness.go](../../internal/lidar/l3grid/background_closeness.go) so the measurement exercises the shipped expression rather than a copy of it.

**Two corrections to the row before the result.** First, the shipped `noise_relative` is **0.02**, not the 0.01 the row assumed, so every figure below is twice what it predicted. Second, the row's proposed remedy watches for the wrong symptom: classification accepts an observation as background when `cellDiff <= closenessThreshold`, so an over-wide threshold absorbs foreground into the background. It causes **missed** detections, not the "false foreground" the row suggests looking for.

The threshold is `multiplier * (spread + noiseRelative*range + 0.01) + safety`, shipped as multiplier 3, safety 0.15 m. Taking the sensor's own specified accuracy as the measured spread term, to isolate the modelled one:

| Range | Modelled noise term | Sensor spec | Ratio | Threshold | Spec-faithful threshold | Inflation |
| ----- | ------------------- | ----------- | ----- | --------- | ----------------------- | --------- |
| 5 m   | 0.10 m              | 0.02 m      | 5×    | 0.54 m    | 0.30 m                  | 1.8×      |
| 20 m  | 0.40 m              | 0.02 m      | 20×   | 1.44 m    | 0.30 m                  | 4.8×      |
| 50 m  | 1.00 m              | 0.03 m      | 33×   | 3.27 m    | 0.36 m                  | 9.1×      |
| 100 m | 2.00 m              | 0.03 m      | 67×   | 6.27 m    | 0.36 m                  | 17.4×     |

**The over-conservatism is not a constant safety factor — it grows linearly with range**, because a relative model is being used where the sensor's accuracy is flat. That is the finding, and it is why "conservative is safe" understates the cost. At 100 m a return must differ from its cell's learned background range by more than **6.27 m** — longer than a car — before it registers as foreground at all. The noise model, not the sensor and not the clustering that follows, sets the long-range detection floor.

Each of those is asserted, so a tuning change that alters the picture fails the suite.

#### Measured against deployed grids

The table above substitutes the sensor spec for the measured spread term. [lidar-closeness-audit](../../cmd/tools/lidar-closeness-audit/main.go) replaces that assumption with the real thing, decoding every `lidar_bg_snapshot` and applying `l3grid.ClosenessThresholdMetres` — the same function the classification loop calls, so the audit cannot drift from the code it audits — to each settled cell:

```bash
go run ./cmd/tools/lidar-closeness-audit -db sensor_data.db
```

Three things the arithmetic alone could not have said.

**The sensor in situ beats its own specification, by a wide margin.** Median measured spread is **6 mm** at close range and only **47 mm** past 100 m, against a specified ±20 mm / ±30 mm. Settled background cells are far more stable than the manual promises, so the relative noise term is not compensating for observed instability — there is very little to compensate for.

**The model supplies almost the entire window; the measurement supplies almost none.** The modelled term is 86% of the bracketed sum at close range and **98% past 50 m**, leaving the measured spread — the only term carrying real information about how variable a particular cell is — contributing **1.5–5%**. The threshold is, in practice, a function of range and nothing else. That makes the per-cell spread the background model works to maintain (an EMA of absolute deviation, not a Welford variance as an earlier revision of this paragraph said; see B1 and B6) nearly irrelevant to the decision it feeds.

**The real inflation is worse than the spec-substituted estimate**, precisely because measured spreads are smaller than the spec: 1.9× at close range rising to **22.7×** past 100 m, and the median window reaches **1.11 m at 10–20 m** — the band containing most of the roadway — and **8.99 m past 100 m**. (Those two figures include the warmup ramp and the locked-baseline path; see the correction below, which also revises the tail sharply upward.)

#### Correction: the closeness window is not the whole decision

The figures above describe one term. The per-point foreground decision in `foreground.go` accepts an observation as background if **any** of three windows contains it:

```go
isBackgroundLike := isWithinLockedRange ||
	cellDiff <= closenessThreshold ||
	(cellNeighbourConfirm > 0 && neighbourConfirmCount >= cellNeighbourConfirm)
```

so the bar a real return must clear is the **widest** of them, and the closeness figures are a lower bound on it. Two corrections follow, both measured by the same audit after extracting `ForegroundClosenessWindowMetres` and `LockedBaselineWindowMetres` alongside the original:

**The warmup ramp was missing from the first pass.** A cell observed fewer than 100 times has its window widened up to 4×, on the reasoning that its learned spread is not yet trustworthy. Between **8.8% and 27.8%** of cells are inside that ramp in any given snapshot, so it is not a startup-only concern.

**The locked-baseline window is nearly universal but rarely binding.** 93–99% of settled cells carry a locked baseline, yet it is the wider of the two windows for only **3.4–11.6%** of them. The reason is worth stating because it is the opposite of what the parameters suggest: the locked window multiplies its spread by 4 against closeness's 3, but it does **not** scale the range-proportional noise term at all, so wherever range dominates — which the model share table shows is everywhere — it comes out narrower. The locked path therefore does not mitigate this gap, and a test pins that relationship so a change to either formula surfaces.

With both included, the effective window (`eff`, the wider of the two per cell):

#### The p90 tail is not a feedback loop

The wide-spread cells behind that tail had three candidate explanations — sky, vegetation, or a loop in which a cell that repeatedly sees traffic learns a wide spread from the traffic itself and is then less able to report the next vehicle. Only the third would be a defect. `-by-ring` attributes them, using ring elevation to identify beams that cannot reach the roadway and `RecentForegroundCount` to identify cells that have actually been seeing traffic:

```bash
go run ./cmd/tools/lidar-closeness-audit -db sensor_data.db -by-ring
```

**The loop is disconfirmed as the mechanism**, on three independent counts.

The decisive one is an inversion. Rings 31–34 (elevation −7.7° to −10.7°, the steep beams that strike the roadway close in) have the **highest** share of foreground-reporting cells, 11.2–12.6%, and the **lowest** share of high-spread cells, 0.5–1.0%. The rings that see the most traffic have the fewest wide-spread cells — the opposite of what the loop predicts. Near-horizon rings, which see the least traffic, run 4–8× higher at 2.2–5.1%.

Second, the foreground share among high-spread cells is flat at **2.9–9.4%** across every range band, and does not rise where high spread concentrates. If traffic were teaching these cells their spread, the two would track.

Third, **38.7%** of the 563,393 high-spread cells sit above the horizon, where the beam never reaches the roadway at all.

What the tail actually is: a long-range phenomenon. High spread as a share of each band rises monotonically with range, from **0.30% at 0–10 m to 23.27% at 75–100 m**. Nearly a quarter of cells past 75 m carry a learned spread over 1 m, which at the shipped multiplier of 3 contributes more than 3 m to the window _on top of_ the 2 m model term — and that is what produces the 45 m p90. The cause is oblique, distant, low-reflectivity returns and genuinely variable scene content, not traffic.

**A caveat this analysis also surfaced, which applies to the tables above.** The cell population is dominated by the near field: **66.0%** of settled cells sit within 10 m and only **1.6%** past 75 m. Every ring's median range is 2–6 m, including the steep down-looking ones, which is not consistent with ground returns at a plausible mounting height — this sensor is seeing close clutter across most of its 360° azimuth sweep, with the roadway in a sector of it. The range-banded arithmetic is unaffected, since it bands by range. But "24 million cells" is not 24 million cells looking at traffic, and the per-band cell counts should be read as the population of the grid rather than of the scene.

**Still not established: the detection cost.** A wide window only loses a detection when a foreground return's range falls within it of the learned background range, and that depends on scene geometry this audit does not model. The window is measured; the miss rate is not. That needs instrumented replay counting near-miss classifications against known foreground, and it remains the open follow-up.

The candidate remedy is a range-banded noise term matching the manual (±2 cm to 30 m, ±3 cm beyond) instead of a fraction of range — but it is a tuning change, so it moves the config fingerprint and requires a baseline recapture, and it should not be made before the replay says what it buys.

#### What the 2026-09 campaign adds

Three things, none of which settles the detection cost.

**Narrowing the window has no measured settling cost.** The obvious risk in the candidate remedy was a grid that settles slower or less stably once it stops absorbing everything nearby. Across 24 sites, `noise_relative` at 0.01 and 0.005 (half and a quarter of the shipped window) regressed settling at no site under the campaign's rule, with one possible exception at one unstable site; see B7. The sensitivity is all on the widening side.

**The detection-cost experiment is now queued, in a weak form.** The fifth pass sweeps `noise_relative` (0.005, 0.05, 0.065) and `closeness_multiplier` (1.5, 5) against labelled tracks on kirk0 and kirk1. Two limits on what it can show. The evaluator sees track existence only (M5), so a vehicle detected later or as fewer points is invisible to it. And both captures' traffic is near-field, where the inflation is 1.9–4.2×, not the 8–23× of the long-range bands where this finding bites. A null result there would not clear the remedy's main case. The instrumented replay described above, counting near-miss classifications by range band, remains the experiment that answers the question.

**The two keys should be treated as one** when that experiment is designed; see B7.

**Later the same day, two results.** Against labelled tracks the window turned out to be the most consequential L3 setting the campaign has found: `closeness_multiplier` 1.5 / 2 / 2.5 / 3 (shipped) / 4 / 5 recovers 13 / 13 / 10 / 7 / 5 / 4 of 16 on kirk0 and 22 / 33 / 17 / 10 / 2 / 2 of 49 on kirk1, monotone at both, with 40–80% more candidate tracks at the narrow end. That is the detection cost this section could not establish, in near-field traffic, through an evaluator that sees only track existence (M5). And the audit above is an underestimate: it applied the configured `noise_relative` of 0.02, but settled cells run at 0.06–0.16 (B8). The tables in this section are the window the config asks for, not the window production uses.
