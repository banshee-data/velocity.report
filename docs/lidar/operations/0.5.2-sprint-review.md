## Findings that change the remaining work

### R1. A medoid is currently labelled as a body centre

**High priority; verified in source.**
[measurementForMode](../../../internal/lidar/l5tracks/measurement.go) selects `medoid_v0` by
default. [SolidBodyFromTrack](../../../internal/lidar/l5tracks/solid_body_lifecycle.go)
sets `ReferenceBodyCentre` whenever `ObservationCount > 0`, with a comment assuming the OBB-centre
measurement. [SampleFromTrack](../../../internal/lidar/l8behaviour/adapter.go) carries that
reference into `ProjectBody`, whose physical-reference check then accepts it.

The downstream production gate is still protective because this adapter never emits `final`.
It would be unsafe to complete the vertical slice by merely changing that stage. Repair anchor
semantics first: medoid and partial OBB centre remain non-physical unless a declared, supported
body correction supplies their offset. Check association, initialisation, updates, persistence,
and rendering against the same anchor contract.

The same adapter loses absence detail: `SupportStateFromL5` reduces support to observed, coasted,
or split, and does not forward `Support.Instant`'s `occluded_inferred`, `missed_unknown`, or
`out_of_fov`. Preserve those reasons and the original acquisition/last-observed time when adding
the final trajectory adapter. Do not infer observation from a missing row or a zero miss count.

### R2. Final Cartesian rows cannot supply the promised body trajectory

**High priority; verified in source.**
[TrackEstimate](../../../internal/lidar/storage/sqlite/state_estimate_store.go) stores position,
velocity and 4×4 covariance, but no body reference/offset, extent belief, orientation uncertainty,
face support, lifecycle or state-model discriminator. The
[refined sink](../../../internal/lidar/pipeline/refined_estimate_sink.go) writes only observed,
confirmed states; coasted states remain in memory. The near-edge model is an offline candidate,
and the filter still uses the scalar measurement variance.

The MVP needs a versioned body trajectory, including unsupported intervals, before it can feed
headway or faithful trails. Reconstructing extents from a final track summary would apply later
knowledge to every frame without recording the revision. `final` describes an inference stage;
it is not an accuracy certificate or a run-completeness watermark.

### R3. Coverage refusal broke an existing PCAP acceptance path

**High priority; reproduced.** #611's
[occlusionContinuityFor](../../../internal/lidar/replayeval/replayeval.go) refuses
`coast_support`, `class_coast_bounds`, and `occlusion_continuity`. There is no explicit coverage
input in that experiment path that can satisfy the refusal. The unchanged
`TestOcclusionContinuityExperimentsOnKirk0` fails on its second arm with hydrated kirk0.
It is excluded from `-race` builds, so a passing race suite does not exercise this regression.

The refusal is appropriate; removing it would restore an unsupported interpretation. Supply
calibrated sensor origin/coverage and a testable coverage contract, then test refusal and
qualified execution separately. `coast_time_inflation`, `reacquisition_guard`,
`capture_gap_predict`, and `measurement_time` remain separable experiments, not promoted defaults.
The old #603 label-free results also predate the exact solver and must remain historical.

### R4. Scene containment prevents overcounting but silently loses boundary encounters

**High priority for the field distribution; verified in source.**
[Scene-window readers](../../../internal/lidar/storage/sqlite/interaction_store.go)
require the entire encounter to lie inside the scene. An encounter spanning either boundary,
including one covering the entire scene, is excluded rather than clipped. Source discovery uses
the same filter, so it can return `no_encounters` despite relevant stored data.

This is a conservative containment policy, not a complete scene-window distribution. Pin an
explicit scene/source/run link; intersect instant intervals with the scene window and recompute
windowed exposure, minima and summaries. Do not reuse an untrimmed encounter minimum or Monte
Carlo interval after clipping. Until then, label the result as fully contained encounters only.
The behaviour plan's previous overlap description no longer matched the code.

**Status:** clipping is done (`dd/lidar/scene-window-clipping`). The scene readers return every
encounter overlapping the window, and the scene headway API clips each crossing encounter to it,
recomputing its accounting, windows and measurements from the instants inside; one it cannot
recompute exactly is left out and named. Source discovery therefore finds a source whose encounters
cross the window. The explicit scene/source/run link remains.

### R5. Encounter totals are not unique follower opportunity

**High priority before promotion; verified in source.**
[analyseFollower](../../../internal/lidar/l8behaviour/encounter.go) builds encounters from
`PairingDecision.Involved()`. A refused path or free-flow decision can produce no encounter;
ambiguity can place the same follower instant in more than one encounter. The
[stored distribution](../../../internal/lidar/l8behaviour/distribution.go) sums
encounter accounting.

Its bins-plus-excluded invariant is sound for that population. It does not prove that every
follower second is represented once, or that a scene with little supported data was observed
well. Preserve follower timelines and report mutually exclusive valid, suppressed, predicted,
free-flow, and missing-input opportunity before producing rates across leader changes. Retain
pair-level summaries as a distinct population.

### R6. The held-out gap scorer is a component, not a complete promotion gate

**High priority before promotion; verified in source.**
[ScoreHeldOut](../../../internal/lidar/l8behaviour/heldout.go) pins the scoring plan and a
reference-set name, but not a digest of the reference content or an object-disjoint split. It
reports unmatched/unscorable references without including those counts in its overall verdict;
only created strata determine pass/fail. Thus good results on a surviving subset do not prove
adequate recall, correct leader choice or complete opportunity. The references are keyed to
estimated track IDs and do not independently declare the true leader at every instant.

Freeze reviewed reference contents and identity mapping, reference uncertainty, expected strata,
and independent pair/no-leader truth. Add eligibility/coverage and wrong-leader checks to the
gate. Reuse #598's identity evaluation; do not replace independent body geometry with the mask
centre or tune bounds against a held-out result. Minimum sample counts should reflect independent
encounters, not thousands of correlated frames from one pair.

**Status:** partly done (`dd/lidar/heldout-gate-v2`, method `following_heldout_scoring_v2`). The
plan pins the reference set's content digest; the unmatched and unscorable shares fail the report
past their bounds; each stratum also needs a minimum number of distinct leader-follower pairs.
Frozen reviewed contents, object-disjoint splits, expected strata, reference uncertainty and
independent leader truth with wrong-leader checks remain.

A statistical review of v2 (Euler, October 2026) found it sound in what it counts. Each reference
is counted once, unscorable references are a subset of matched ones, and the content digest is
order-independent and deterministic across architectures. The review then found four ways the
gate is weaker than R6 asks. The first is partly fixed; the rest need leader truth or a
statistical design choice:

1. **Leaving an instant unevaluated was cheaper than suppressing it.** An instant the estimator
   never evaluated (an ambiguous leader, a party without a row) had no point, so its references
   became unmatched in the pooled share instead of suppressed in their stratum. With 1,000
   references, a stratum of 80 with half suppressed fails a 0.2 suppression bound; declaring those
   40 instants ambiguous moved them to a 4% pooled unmatched share, under a 0.10 bound, and the
   stratum disappeared. **Partly fixed in v2:** an unevaluated instant is now a suppression in its
   stratum under its reason, once per case however many leaders competed, so declaring an instant
   ambiguous costs what suppressing it costs. Still to do: compute the unmatched and unscorable
   shares per stratum from reference-derived keys, and have the plan list its expected strata.
2. **Every bound is tested on a point estimate.** Frames within one encounter are nearly perfectly
   correlated, so the effective sample is close to the number of encounters.
   - **p95 error.** With an endpoint sigma of 0.177 m (true p95 0.35 m), the estimated p95 has a
     95% interval of roughly [0.23, 0.47] m at 30 independent encounters and [0.15, 0.55] m at
     10, so either straddles a 0.35 m bound. A distribution-free 95% upper bound on a p95 needs
     at least 59 independent units.
   - **Coverage.** At 30 encounters and a true coverage of 0.9, the Wilson interval is
     [0.74, 0.97]. An honest estimator then fails a [0.8, 0.97] range about 7% of the time per
     stratum, which is about 75% of the time somewhere across 20 strata.
   - **Fix:** a seeded bootstrap over whole encounters. Pass when the upper bound is inside the
     error bound and the coverage interval is inside its range; fail when the interval is
     outside; otherwise call it insufficient, with a multiple-comparison correction across strata.
3. **An encounter is a pair of estimated track ids.** Fragmentation inflates the count, and one
   leader followed by three cars contributes the same length error three times. The floor
   refuses too few pairs but does not reweight, so one 1,000-frame encounter and one 1-frame
   encounter meet a floor of 2 with an effective count near 1. Fix: give references a physical
   object id, count bodies for endpoint strata and body pairs for gap strata, and gate on the
   Kish effective count.
4. **The unmatched share is not a miss rate.** Its denominator includes bodies that were never in
   a pair, so its bound must be loose. A wrong-leader pair can score an accurate gap to the wrong
   body while the true leader shows only as unmatched. Fix: restrict the denominator to references
   in a true pair, which needs leader truth.

The same review's smaller findings are also fixed in v2: non-finite or duplicate estimates are refused,
references are validated before their digest, the plan hash covers the method id, and an
insufficient stratum names the bounds it breaks. No default values exist for
`MinEncountersPerStratum`, `MaxUnmatchedRate` or `MaxUnscorableRate`. They are plan inputs to be
set and pinned with their rationale before a held-out set is scored; the fixtures' values of 1
admit everything.

### R7. Uncertainty and smoothing evidence still forbid promotion

**High priority; source and replay evidence.** Baseline NIS is labelled
`accepted_associations_only`, not the eligible pre-gate, rank-specific population required by
G-UNC-1. The endpoint equations use independence assumptions; the encounter Monte Carlo uses a
configured common-mode fraction and marginal Gaussian draws. These are useful methods whose
calibration remains unproved, particularly near zero speed and for extrema.

The fresh kirk0 run again fails the G-SMO-1 revision bound at every horizon. A smoother reducing
speed steps while slightly increasing lateral residual is not a physical correction. Fix the
measurement and calibrate Q/R first; then compare horizons and genuine manoeuvres without changing
the acceptance thresholds after seeing results.

### R8. Storage durability is stronger than live capture completeness

**Medium priority for offline MVP; required before promoting live capture.** #609 commits received
L4 frames independently of L5 and recovers its durable prefix. It cannot describe frames lost
before the tap without an L2 drop producer. It closes on replay/retune instead of rolling into a
new capture; #611 drains queued callbacks but does not add rollover, clock epochs or the phase-3
worker. The UI needs separate captured, processed and final frontiers.

The PR's opening claim that frames are committed before L5 reads them is too broad. In
[AppendFrame](../../../internal/lidar/storage/vrlog/writer.go), success means **accepted**; only
strict mode waits for durability. The pipeline calls `tap.finish` and then `Tracker.Update`
without waiting for the group-commit frontier. Thus the current direct tracker can process
undurable observations. The existing phase-2 architecture documentation correctly distinguishes
append from commit. A future frontier-consuming worker, or the MVP's verified closed-log reader,
must establish the stronger input guarantee before its results can be published as durable.

`TestShedFramesBecomeExplicitGaps` also fails locally: the test assumes that releasing the
committer makes frame 4 admissible within 20 ms, but that frame is shed. All ten isolated
repetitions failed. Investigate the completion synchronisation and storage-latency assumption;
do not claim a clean VRLOG suite or dismiss this as proof of evidence corruption. The fidelity,
process-kill recovery and corruption-refusal cases passed in the same run.

### R9. The sensor-clock fix is narrower than global monotonicity

**Medium priority; verified in source.** #611 fixes within-second interpretation and resets the
PTP/GPS fallback when the combined timestamp changes. It does not compare against the last emitted
time: fallback to host time, return to a differently offset sensor, or a sensor clock reset can
still step backwards. Even a changed but earlier sensor timestamp resets the static counter.
The parser tests prove the ordinary second rollover and fallback release, not clock discipline.

PCAP replay overrides these modes with packet capture timestamps, so passing a PCAP test cannot
validate live sensor-clock recovery. Keep clock epochs/discontinuities explicit and suppress
unsupported intervals. The mode table and remaining-work list are corrected in the
[time-domain model](../architecture/time-domain-model.md).

### R10. The requested debug layer does not exist

**MVP requirement, not a cosmetic follow-up.** The
[visualiser protocol](../../../proto/velocity_visualiser/v1/visualiser.proto) has association,
gating, residual and prediction overlays, but no following gap, endpoint, pair or estimate-stage
payload. The native renderer has a velocity toggle; the headway scene histogram is a separate
surface. Add a Following distance layer using authoritative per-instant values and persisted path
geometry, with individual-measure inspection and seek/version correctness. Its contract is in the
[sprint plan](../../plans/lidar-052-mvp-sprint-plan.md#following-distance-debug-layer).

## Corrections to the supplied earlier review

Its central conclusion is right: the merged batch is not a completed physical estimator. Its
remaining-work list needs the following qualifications:

- E1.2/E1.4 were run on Columbus on September 19. The stale plan/backlog text is corrected; the
  [dated experiment record](state-estimation-e1-lateral-error.md) remains intact. Three qualifying
  stationary candidate tracks do not establish multi-site uncertainty calibration.
- Final-stage rows already exist through #605. The missing product is a complete, qualified body
  trajectory and coherent publication, rather than final rows of any kind.
- S3 likelihood, S2 cascade and extent experiments already have code. Selection, integration and
  held-out validation remain; the exact solver is delivered, not another open implementation task.
- #611 closed the previously listed medium follow-ups, while introducing the coverage/test mismatch
  and changing the scene population. Repeating #603's old command is no longer a viable next step.
- The claimed fresh Columbus rerun in the supplied text has no attached manifest or hash here.
  It is not adopted as verified evidence. This review's fresh replay is kirk0, explicitly scoped.
- Pi optimisation and live power-loss qualification do not block a qualified offline Mac result.
  They remain required for their respective deployment/capture claims. They must not disappear
  from the backlog merely to make the sprint look smaller.

The sprint can deliver an integrated, visibly provisional MVP first. Promotion is a separate exit
requiring the existing physical gates and the metric-specific reference/coverage checks. Neither
an attractive distance arrow nor a successful synthetic PDF changes that boundary.
