# City view: every site with data on one city map, in the visualiser and the web scenes

A whole-city view in the macOS visualiser and in the published web scenes. It shows the street grid,
the buildings, and which sites have data. A reader zooms into an intersection to play its recording,
and moves to another site with data while the city stays drawn around it. Everything the view needs
comes from static files: no new server endpoints.

- **Status:** Draft (D-32: not counted as active; the owner places it in a release slot)
- **Decisions:** D-32 (planning limits), D-33 (hub doc states what ships), D-34 (scene assets leave
  the repository), D-38 (geometry wording); Gerardus
  [ADR-0011](https://github.com/banshee-data/gerardus/blob/main/docs/adr/0011-publish-snapshots-and-aggregates-not-trails.md)
  (per-object tracks never leave the sensor site)
- **Layers:** macOS visualiser; web scene player (`public_html/`); offline scene tooling
  (`tools/s2-archive/`, `tools/s2-hilbert/`)
- **Target:** unscheduled; owner decision, 2026-11-04
- **Companion plans:** [lidar-web-scene-export-plan](lidar-web-scene-export-plan.md) (scene format),
  [lidar-scene-catalogue-publishing-plan](lidar-scene-catalogue-publishing-plan.md) (which scenes
  exist), [lidar-visualiser-gerardus-alignment-plan](lidar-visualiser-gerardus-alignment-plan.md)
  (accepted poses); Gerardus
  [CITY-VIEW-TILES](https://github.com/banshee-data/gerardus/blob/main/docs/plans/CITY-VIEW-TILES.md)
  (the city tiles both clients read)
- **Canonical:** [city-view.md](../ui/city-view.md)

The city tiles carry measured spatial data: the City's building footprints with heights from its
2010 aerial survey, the City's street centrelines, and 3DEP ground, each at its own acquisition
time. They give context for the traffic and are not a plan, survey or specification of any street.
See [Geometry priors: data purpose](../geometry-priors-data-purpose.md).

## Motivation

The project now records across San Francisco, not at one junction. Both clients are still built
around one site at a time:

- The public scene map is a flat marker map. A reader cannot see a street or a building on it.
- Each scene page plays in its own sensor frame, and nothing about it shows where it sits in the
  city.
- The visualiser draws one sensor's frame. Changing site means changing server or run, and the app
  works out which site it is showing from file names.

Without a city view a reader cannot tell where the data is, what lies between two sites, or how one
survey relates to the next. The owner wants both clients to give a whole-city view, at
2026-11-04:

1. see where we have data;
2. zoom in to those intersections;
3. move between sites with data while still seeing the city grid and buildings;
4. with level of detail and simplification so this stays smooth.

## Current state

**Web scenes** (`public_html/`):

- `public_html/src/js/scene-map.js` is a 2D SVG map, zoom 12–16, with the SF coastline
  (`public_html/src/geo/sf-coastline.geojson`) and an opt-in third-party cycle-map layer
  (`scene-map-mode.js`). Its markers come from `public_html/src/_data/scenes.json`. That file is
  generated by `tools/s2-hilbert/generate-scene-map.mjs` and grouped at S2 L10, L13 and L16. It
  lists 24 located scenes and 1 unlocated scene.
- Each scene plays in the three.js player in its sensor frame (`scene-coords.js`: ENU mapped to
  three.js Y-up). `tools/s2-archive/scene-vectors.py` adds Gerardus's geometry within 90 m of the
  sensor, and only for a site with an accepted pose. It places the geometry by the inverse pose, and
  says outright that "nothing georeferenced is added".
- three.js is vendored as `public_html/src/vendor/three` → `node_modules/three/build`. The addons
  (`GLTFLoader`) are not vendored.

**macOS visualiser** (`tools/visualiser-macos/`):

- One velocity.report server and one stream at a time (`App/VelocityReportServer.swift:19-52`,
  `App/AppState.swift:712-754`). One server is one pipeline and one sensor
  (`internal/cmd/server/radar.go:558,671-675`).
- The site is inferred after the fact from clip and capture names
  (`Alignment/AlignmentSiteLookup.swift:77-133`). The heading is typed in by hand, because no API
  serves `north_azimuth_deg` (`AlignmentSiteLookup.swift:11-12`).
- Everything is drawn in the sensor frame in Float32. The far plane is 500 m and zoom is clamped to
  1–500 m (`Rendering/MetalRenderer.swift:1502,1813`).
- Gerardus geometry is streamed for one L16 site within 150 m (`AlignmentSession.swift:223`), with a
  single `referenceModelMatrix`.
- `FrameBundle` carries `sensor_id` and no site (`proto/velocity_visualiser/v1/visualiser.proto`).
  `CoordinateFrameInfo` has origin fields that only synthetic mode fills in
  (`internal/lidar/l9endpoints/model.go:442-451`).
- State a site switch must reset is held in five places: `AppState`, the renderer's background
  cache, `AlignmentSession`, `StreetPainterSession`, and annotation packs. `AppState.clearAll`
  deliberately keeps the background grid.

**Gerardus:**

- Reference geometry is served per L16 site: a bundle of the cell and its 8 neighbours, about 420 m
  square, prepared on demand.
- The City's footprints (with `hgt_median_m` and `gnd_min_m` from 2010 lidar) and street centrelines
  are already cached per L13 on its data directory (`footprints/l13/`, `streets/l13/`).
- There is no city-wide export. The city buildings bake (CITY-BUILDINGS) is planned and has not
  been run.

**Poses:** every one of the 24 published scenes has an accepted Gerardus pose. Each scene's
`assets/vectors/vectors.geojson.gz` names the record in its `placement` block (`record_id`,
`registration_state`, `pose_source`, `heading_deg`, `up_sensor`, and the 2 m / 5° check it
passed):

- 6 are `registered`, accepted from a candidate that passed every gate: ashbury-downey,
  columbus-broadway, columbus-north-point, embarcadero-folsom, laguna-eddy and lombard-laguna.
- 18 are `manually_placed`: an operator accepted the pose, each from an ICP result

The block holds no translation. `scene-vectors.py` reads it from Gerardus at export time

`assets/placement.json` carries the full pose and the transform into the city frame. Gerardus's
`REGISTRATION-STATUS.md` still describes 2026-10-24 and says none is accepted; it is out of date.

## Design / approach

### What the reader sees

- **Zoomed out:** the whole city: the street grid with street names, buildings drawn simply, and a
  marker at every site with data. A marker shows what the site has (a published scene, or a capture
  the connected server can replay) and its recording date.
- **Zooming in:** buildings and streets gain detail tile by tile. Sites in view start playing in
  place, each inside its own coverage, with the city drawn around them. The site the reader is
  closest to plays in full: points, boxes and its scene vectors.
- **Moving between sites:** click another marker, or choose next and previous. The camera rises,
  crosses the city and comes down at the new site. Sites that leave the view are unloaded.

### Several sites at once, each kept to itself

Several sites may play at once (owner, 2026-11-04), and the view should keep working as sites
become denser. The view must make clear that we are **not tracking traffic between intersections**:
each site is its own recording, and nothing joins one site's road users to another's. This is how
the data is made, and it is also the privacy rule that per-object tracks never leave the sensor site
(Gerardus ADR-0011, SPEC-V1 §1).

How the view makes that visible:

1. **Coverage, not corridors.** A site's points and boxes are drawn only inside its coverage: the
   sensor's range disc, clipped to the scene's export radius. A faint ring marks its edge. A box
   fades out as it reaches the ring, and nothing is drawn on the streets between sites.
2. **Own clock, own date.** Each site plays its own recording on its own clock, labelled with its
   date and time of day. Sites recorded on different days are never presented as the same moment.
3. **No identity across sites.** Colour is by class (vehicle, cycle, walking), never by track.
   There are no trails, arrows or lines from one site to another. Track ids are namespaced by site
   in both clients, so code cannot join them by accident. A test asserts that no rendered object
   draws data from two sites.
4. **Said in words.** The page and the visualiser's city mode carry one line of copy: "Each
   intersection is recorded on its own. Vehicles are not followed from one intersection to the
   next."

The web view composes scenes that are already published, so it publishes nothing new. A city-wide
layer of aggregates (counts and speeds per segment, no bin under k = 5) would also be allowed, but
it waits on the traffic v1 network and is not part of this plan.

### Scene level of detail

As many sites come into view, playback is simplified by distance so the frame budget holds:

| Range         | What plays                                       | Budget        |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | ------------- |
| Nearest site  | Points, boxes, background and scene vectors      | one site      |
| Street range  | Boxes and headings only, from the scene's frames | up to 8 sites |
| District/city | Marker with the site's date; no playback         | every site    |

The limits are starting values, to be measured in Items 3 and 7.

### Inputs: three kinds of static file, no new endpoints

1. **City tiles** (Gerardus, companion plan). This is a 3D Tiles 1.1 explicit `tileset.json` with
   one binary glTF (`.glb`) per S2 cell at three levels of detail. It holds ground, footprints with
   heights, and street centrelines. A Gerardus bake command writes it, and it is served as plain
   files. The visualiser reads it from a URL or a directory (the NAS). The web view reads it from
   the scene asset store (D-34).
2. **Placement sidecar per scene:** `assets/placement.json`, written by
   `tools/s2-archive/publish-scenes.py` from the same accepted record and check as the vectors
   file. It holds:
   - the record id and registration state;
   - the full sensor-to-site pose, rotation and translation, which the vectors file leaves out;
   - the site-to-city transform, computed once in tooling from the site's L16 origin. Clients do no
     geodesy.

   The scene's own files stay in the sensor frame, and the sidecar is the only georeferenced part.
   `scene-vectors.py`'s rule therefore still holds for the scene itself.

3. **City site index:** `city/sites.json`, written by `generate-scene-map.mjs` beside `scenes.json`.
   It lists, for every site:
   - its id, title and L10/L13/L16 tokens;
   - its scene path, capture names and recording date;
   - its placement: record id, state and sensor-to-city matrix.

   The web view and the visualiser read the same file. The visualiser no longer needs a typed
   heading.

The visualiser's live and replay data still comes from the connected velocity.report server, using
existing calls:

- `GET /api/lidar/clip-locations` for what that server can replay;
- `POST /api/lidar/pcap/start` (or VRLOG replay) to start a site's capture.

The alignment pane keeps its per-site Gerardus stream for detailed work at one site.

### Frames and precision

- **City frame:** ENU at a fixed origin that the tileset declares in its root transform. Heights
  are NAVD88 and horizontal positions NAD83(2011), as in Gerardus's site frames.
- **Tile coordinates:** each tile's vertices are Float32 offsets from that tile's origin. Its offset
  from the city origin is stored as a Double in `tileset.json`.
- **Placing a site:** sensor-to-city is site-to-city × sensor-to-site. Both come from the sidecar or
  index in Double.
- **Camera-relative rendering:** clients compute each draw's model-view on the CPU in Double, as
  (tile or site offset − camera position) × rotation, and pass it to the GPU in Float32. Values
  stay small near the camera, which removes the jitter large translations cause in Float32
  matrices.
- **Depth:** the visualiser's 500 m far plane and zoom clamp become dynamic near and far planes
  (reversed-Z in Metal), so a 12 km view and a 5 m view share one renderer. The web view does the
  same with a logarithmic depth buffer.

### Level of detail and simplification

Three tile levels, chosen by each tile's screen-space error. The Gerardus companion plan owns the
exact simplification rules and size budgets.

| Level    | S2 cell (edge) | Ground    | Buildings                                            | Streets                    |
| -------- | -------------- | --------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| City     | L10 (~9 km)    | 50 m grid | Footprints merged per block, one height each         | Arterials only, simplified |
| District | L13 (~1 km)    | 10 m grid | Every footprint over a minimum area, simplified      | All active streets         |
| Street   | L16 (~150 m)   | 2 m grid  | Every footprint at full detail with its height       | All, full detail           |
| Site     | (scene)        | (scene)   | Scene vectors near the sensor; city tiles fade there | Scene vectors              |

**Street names** are drawn as labels from the tiles (owner, 2026-11-04): arterials at L10, all
named streets at L13 and L16. They are the City's own names (DataSF, PDDL), placed along each
centreline. Labels are for finding your way around. They are not attributes of any feature, and the
tiles carry no addresses, building names or parcel numbers.

Client rules:

- Each tile is one mesh per layer, so it costs two or three draw calls.
- An LRU cache keeps the tiles near the camera.
- A loading tile shows its parent until it arrives.
- Within a played site's vector radius (90 m), city buildings fade so that the scene's measured
  walls and roofs are not drawn twice.

### Placement only from accepted poses

All 24 sites have an accepted pose, so every site is placed from its record. A new site appears as
a marker at its eye-read position, labelled "not yet placed", until a pose is accepted for it. It
does not play in the city until then, and opens in its own frame as scene pages do today. A guess is
never drawn as though it were a measured placement.

### Web specifics

- **Where:** a new `/city/` route (owner, 2026-11-04). The 2D scene map stays: it is the fallback
  without WebGL, under reduced motion, and on low-power devices.
- **Reuse:** `scene-reader.js` and `scene-playback.js` for the site's frames, `scene-flight.js` and
  `scene-camera.js` for the transitions, and `scene-vectors.js` for the site's vectors.
- **Tile loader:** a small loader reads `tileset.json`, selects tiles by screen-space error and
  loads `.glb` files with three.js's `GLTFLoader`, newly vendored from `three/examples/jsm`. No 3D
  Tiles library is added.
- **Deep links:** `/city/#site=columbus-broadway` opens at a site.
- **D-34:** the tiles live with the scene assets in the external store. If the public site has moved
  to its own repository by then, the page goes with it.

### Visualiser specifics

- **`SiteContext`:** one object owns everything held per site: the alignment session, reference
  buffers, background cache, street painter and annotation frame. Switching site swaps one object
  instead of resetting five places.
- **City mode:** a camera mode over the city tiles, with markers from `city/sites.json`. Choosing a
  site, or next/previous, works like this:
  1. stop the current stream and swap the `SiteContext`;
  2. if the connected server is that site's live sensor, attach to it; otherwise start a replay of
     one of the site's captures with `POST /api/lidar/pcap/start`;
  3. fly the camera to the site;
  4. draw the stream in place by the site's sensor-to-city matrix.
- **Other sites in view:** the server plays one replay at a time, so the other sites in view are
  played from their published scene exports. A Swift reader for the scene format reads them
  directly from files, at the street-range level of detail (boxes and headings). Live Pis in the
  field are each a separate server, and the visualiser may hold one stream per live site.
- **Sites with nothing to play:** markers, with their published scene's details.
- **Tile reader:** a Swift reader for `tileset.json` plus a minimal `.glb` reader (one JSON chunk
  and one binary chunk, with positions and indices only; no normals, Item 2), feeding Metal
  buffers directly.
- **Settings:** `cityTiles.location` (URL or directory) for the tiles, and `citySites.location`
  for the site index, next to `alignment.serviceAddress`. The index is published with the scenes
  and the tiles by Gerardus, so the two are kept apart rather than one derived from the other.
  A site with its own Pi in the field has its live server kept on the Mac
  (`cityView.liveServer.<site id>`), never in a published file.

**As built (Item 5):** `SiteContext` (`App/SiteContext.swift`) holds the site and its tracks; a
switch makes a new one, and the renderer (`leaveSite`: stream, background cache, reference,
residuals, street marks), `AlignmentSession` (`leaveSite`: placement, reference, scans, records,
refinement) and, through the alignment's reference, `StreetPainterSession` drop the old site's
state. The annotation window takes the site id from it for its map-marks line. The toolbar's site
picker lists `city/sites.json` with Previous and Next; a switch matches the site's captures (else
its L16 cell) against `GET /api/lidar/clip-locations` and the clip's files, and replays the ones
the index names with `POST /api/lidar/pcap/start`. The alignment pane resolves the open capture
from the index before file names, and takes the heading from it. Tests in
`SiteContextTests.swift` switch away and back and check that no track, background, reference or
street mark survives. The fixtures in `testdata/city-view/` are three entries of Item 1's
`city/sites.json`, one scene's `placement.json` and Gerardus's tile fixture. The index's
`heading_deg` (the bearing of the sensor's +Y) is the hint heading; the pane shows it as the
north azimuth it would have typed, 180° − heading.

**As built (Item 6):** `RenderOrigin` (`Rendering/RenderOrigin.swift`) keeps every placed
drawable's offset in Double and draws in a local frame anchored near the camera; each draw's model
matrix is (offset − anchor) in Double, then Float32, and in city mode the anchor follows the
camera (recentred past 1 km, whole metres). A site's sensor frame never recentres and draws as
before. Depth is reversed (near 1, far 0, cleared to 0, `.greater`) for both projections, a
perspective view sets its clip planes from the eye's distance to its target (near 0.02–20 m, far
at least 600 m and 40 × the distance + 3 km), and zoom runs from 1 m to 60 km: the 500 m far plane
and zoom clamp are gone. `City/CityTileset.swift` and `City/GLBReader.swift` read the tiles to the
profile above (Y-up turned to Z-up; `uint8`, `uint16` or `uint32` indices; gzip on disk
unwrapped). `City/TileSelection.swift` selects by screen-space error (16 px), REPLACE refinement
with a loading tile's parent drawn in its place and frustum culling, and keeps loaded tiles in a
256 MB LRU cache that never evicts a tile drawn now. `Rendering/CityTileLayer.swift` and
`Shaders/CityTiles.metal` draw each tile's ground, buildings (flat shaded from `dfdx`/`dfdy`) and
streets: three draw calls a tile. Until city mode (Item 7), the tiles are drawn around a placed
site's sensor by the inverse of its sensor-to-city. `cityTiles.location` (Settings ▸ Servers ▸
City view) takes an epoch directory, a `city-tiles` directory (its `current`, else its newest
epoch, as over SMB the link does not resolve), a `tileset.json`, or a URL. `CityTilesTests.swift`
reads the shared fixture `testdata/city-view/tiles/`, Gerardus's own.

## Scope

### Item 1: placement sidecar and city site index

**Summary:** Every published scene carries `placement.json`, and `city/sites.json` lists every
site with data and its placement.

**Steps:**

1. Write `placement.json` in `publish-scenes.py` from the record and check `scene-vectors.py`
   already uses, adding the translation. Write it for all 24 scenes. A site without an accepted pose
   gets `state: "not_placed"` and its eye-read position, for the marker only.
2. Compute site-to-city in tooling from the L16 origin and the tileset's declared city origin, in
   Double, with a test against Gerardus's GeoJSON origin for one site.
3. Emit `city/sites.json` from `generate-scene-map.mjs`, with CI regenerating it and failing on any
   difference, as for `scenes.json`.
4. Extend the export privacy test so the sidecar and index hold only placement and provenance: no
   tracks, names or addresses.

**Milestone:** unscheduled

### Item 2: city tiles (Gerardus companion)

**Summary:** Gerardus writes the city tiles as static files. This plan only consumes them.

**Steps:**

1. Track the Gerardus CITY-VIEW-TILES plan to its first published epoch.
2. Agree the city origin and the `.glb` profile with that plan before Items 3 and 6 start.
   Agreed as built (Gerardus PR 71, CITY-VIEW-TILES §3 and §8; `gerardus-city-tiles-1`): the city
   origin is lat 37.76, lon −122.44, NAVD88 0. Each `.glb` has at most three meshes of one
   primitive each, `ground` and `buildings` (triangles) and `streets` (lines), with POSITION and
   indices only: **no normals and no materials**. Clients shade buildings flat from each
   triangle's own normal (screen-space derivatives in Metal, `flatShading` in three.js); the
   ground is a shared-vertex grid. Vertices are glTF Y-up, glTF (x, y, z) = city (east, up,
   −north), Float32 metres from the tile's origin, and triangles wind counter-clockwise seen from
   outside. Tile transforms are translations from the parent tile's origin, and every tile also
   gives its own origin in city ENU metres in `extras.origin_city_enu_m`, which clients use
   instead of the root's ECEF transform. Street-name labels are in each tile's
   `extras.labels`.

**Milestone:** unscheduled

### Item 3: web city view, zoomed out

**Summary:** `/city/` draws the city tiles and street names with level of detail, and a marker per
site.

**Steps:**

1. Vendor `GLTFLoader`. Write the tile loader with screen-space-error selection, an LRU cache and
   parent-until-loaded.
2. Add a camera-relative render path and a logarithmic depth buffer, and orbit and zoom from city
   scale to street scale.
3. Draw the street-name labels from the tiles, with collision avoidance.
4. Add markers from `city/sites.json`, showing each site's data kind and recording date, with the 2D
   map as fallback.
5. Set and measure a frame and byte budget on a mid-range phone and a laptop. Record the numbers in
   the hub doc.

**Milestone:** unscheduled

### Item 4: web, sites in place and between sites

**Summary:** Sites in view play in place, each inside its own coverage, and the reader can move to
the next site with data.

**Steps:**

1. Load and play scenes placed by their `placement.json` inside the city tiles, fading city
   buildings within each scene's vector radius.
2. Apply the scene level of detail: full playback for the nearest site, boxes only at street range,
   markers beyond.
3. Add the separation cues: the coverage ring and fade, a per-site clock and date, class colours
   only, track ids namespaced by site, the one line of copy, and a test that no drawn object mixes
   two sites.
4. Add next/previous and a marker click, with a flight that rises, crosses and descends, unloading
   the sites that leave the view.
5. Add `/city/#site=<id>` deep links, and link to `/city/` from each scene page.

**Milestone:** unscheduled

### Item 5: visualiser, switching between sites

**Summary:** A `SiteContext` holds per-site state, and a site list switches the stream between sites
the connected server can play.

**Steps:**

1. Introduce `SiteContext` and move per-site state into it from `AppState`, the renderer,
   `AlignmentSession`, `StreetPainterSession` and annotation.
2. Read `city/sites.json`, and resolve the site from it rather than from file names. Take the
   heading from it.
3. Switch with the existing clip-locations and replay-start calls, or attach to a live sensor.
4. Add tests that switching away and back leaks no tracks, background or reference geometry between
   sites.

**Milestone:** unscheduled

### Item 6: visualiser, city frame and tiles

**Summary:** The renderer draws a 12 km view and a 5 m view without jitter, and reads the city
tiles.

**Steps:**

1. Store anchors in Double, render camera-relative, use reversed-Z with dynamic near and far planes,
   and remove the 500 m clamps.
2. Write the Swift `tileset.json` and `.glb` readers, with fixtures shared with Item 3, and add tile
   selection and an LRU cache.
3. Add the `cityTiles.location` setting.

**Milestone:** unscheduled

### Item 7: visualiser, city mode

**Summary:** The operator sees the city, its street names and the sites, flies to one, plays it and
the sites around it in place, and moves to the next.

**Steps:**

1. Add a city camera mode with markers and street-name labels.
2. Wire choosing a site to Item 5's switch, and fly to it.
3. Draw the stream by its sensor-to-city matrix, fading city buildings near the sensor.
4. Add the separation cues from Item 4, shared through the test fixtures.

**Milestone:** unscheduled

### Item 8: visualiser, other sites from published scenes

**Summary:** The sites around the focused one play from their published scene exports, read from
files, so several sites play at once without new endpoints.

**Steps:**

1. Write a Swift reader for the scene export's manifest, timeline and frames (boxes and headings
   only), tested against a committed scene fixture.
2. Play up to the street-range limit of nearby sites, each on its own clock, placed by its
   `placement.json`.
3. Hold one stream per live Pi when several live sites are in view, each in its own `SiteContext`.

**Milestone:** unscheduled

## Dependencies

- Gerardus CITY-VIEW-TILES: the first tile epoch gates Items 3, 6 and 7. It was baked on
  2026-11-05 (Item 2), and Item 1 is done, so Items 3 to 8 can start.
- Accepted poses: all 24 published sites have one. A new site needs one before it plays in the city
  (Gerardus M2 and M3).
- D-34: where scene assets and tiles are hosted publicly.
- The visualiser's replay of another site needs a server that holds that site's captures (arrow or
  kirk0 with the NAS). A Pi in the field can only play its own site.

## Design decisions

Owner, 2026-11-04:

- Static tiles, kept simple, and no new endpoints. Both clients read files, and live and replay data
  uses existing calls.
- Both the visualiser and the web scene exports get the city view.
- Level of detail and simplification are acceptable to keep it smooth.
- Several sites may play at once, and the view must keep working as sites become denser. It must
  make clear that traffic is not tracked between intersections.
- The web view is a new `/city/` route.
- Street names are labelled.
- Each site plays its own recording on its own clock. Sites are not aligned by time of day,
  which would suggest a shared moment that never happened.
- The visualiser plays archived nearby sites from their published scene exports (Item 8).
- Placement comes from accepted poses only. All 24 published scenes have one (6 `registered`, 18
  `manually_placed`).
