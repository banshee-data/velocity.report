# Directional speed reporting: a limit per lane, speeds by lane and direction, and two-direction histograms on the survey site plan

An intersection signed 25 mph one way and 30 mph the other has two populations of speeds and two limits, and today every report, chart and survey page shows one histogram against one number. This plan takes the travel lanes approved in Gerardus, each with its direction and its own `maxspeed`, into velocity.report as a copy against Gerardus's ids; attributes each vehicle track to a lane and a direction by position and heading; reports speeds per direction against that direction's limit in the PDF, the charts API and the scene export; carries the radar's inbound and outbound through the same names; and gives the survey site two histograms side by side on one scale, each with its own limit line.

- **Status:** Proposed (the owner asked for it on November 3, 2026). Depends on approved lanes with limits in Gerardus ([lanes from tracks](lidar-lane-proposals-from-tracks-plan.md)) for the limits and the names; the split by heading, the radar half and the survey site's layout can start before them; no code is written
- **Decisions:** D-16 (schedules follow the posted limit), D-18 (every published percentile is nearest rank over the population of vehicle maxima, so a direction's p85 is computed as the site's is, from its own population), D-32, D-34 (the survey site leaves the repository: everything here lives in `survey-model.js`, `survey-charts.js` and the export format, so it moves with it), D-38
- **Layers:** a lanes copy (site data), L8 attribution, reports (PDF, `data.json`, the charts API), the scene export (`timeline.json`), radar transits, the public survey site
- **Target:** the posted-limit items in [v0.7.7](../BACKLOG.md#v077---safety-reporting-077) for the limit model; the rest proposed as [v0.7.19](../BACKLOG.md#v0719---lanes-from-tracks-and-directional-speeds-0719)
- **Companion plans:** [Posted speed limits](posted-speed-limits-plan.md) (the limit model; revised to live on lanes), [Speed limit schedules](../radar/architecture/speed-limit-schedules.md), [Speeding attitudes](platform-speeding-attitudes-and-aggressive-driving-plan.md) (limit-relative shares by 5, 10 and 15 mph), [Lanes from tracks](lidar-lane-proposals-from-tracks-plan.md), [Platform vocabulary](platform-vocabulary-and-data-model-plan.md) (the deployment's sensor heading, V32), [Web scene export](lidar-web-scene-export-plan.md), [Lane stitching](lidar-lane-stitching-plan.md)
- **Canonical:** [Percentile aggregation semantics](../radar/architecture/percentile-aggregation-semantics.md)

## Motivation

Speed is reported as one population per site: every vehicle track above 5 mph counts once at its maximum, and the histogram, p50, p85, p98 and the share over the limit come from that one list ([timeline.go](../../internal/scene/timeline.go), the radar rollups, `survey-model.js`). The limit is one number per report request (`ReportConfig.SpeedLimit`) or one `speed_limit_mph` per site in `scenes.json`. Where the two directions of a street carry different limits, half the vehicles are compared with the wrong sign; where they carry the same limit but behave differently (downhill against uphill, towards a signal against away from it), the difference is averaged away. The owner's example is an intersection signed 25 mph one way and 30 mph the other.

The [posted speed limits plan](posted-speed-limits-plan.md) already says a limit belongs to a stretch of road for one direction of travel, optionally one lane, split at its signs, and is signed in a unit. It was parked for want of road geometry to hang it on. Gerardus's travel lanes, proposed from the lane field and approved in the visualiser, are that geometry: each is directed, carries a `maxspeed` with its unit and source, and is named by its street and bearing ([Gerardus LANE-PROPOSALS §5.4–5.5](https://github.com/banshee-data/gerardus/blob/main/docs/plans/LANE-PROPOSALS.md#54-tags-on-a-travel-lane)). This plan is what velocity.report does with them.

## Design

### 1. Lanes and limits reach velocity.report

A **lanes copy** per site, stored here against Gerardus's ids, fetched by an operator action, never required for the device to run (tenet 1: the sensor works without Gerardus or a network).

- **Table** `site_lanes` (new migration): `lane_id` (`gms:` id), `row_id` (the Gerardus version read), `l16_token`, `geometry_site_json` (vertices in the site frame, draped), `record_id` (the accepted pose used to place it), `geometry_sensor_json` (the same vertices in the sensor frame, from that pose), `direction_bearing_deg`, `lane_type`, `width_m`, `maxspeed_value` and `maxspeed_unit` (`mph` or `km/h`; a `CHECK` that both are set or neither), `maxspeed_source`, `jurisdiction`, `street`, `direction_id` (§2), `fetched_at`, `gerardus_version`. Append-only by convention: a new fetch inserts rows with a new `fetched_at`, and a report names the `fetched_at` it used, so a report is reproducible against the lanes that existed when it ran.
- **Fetch**: `velocity lidar lanes pull --site <l16> [--pose <record.json>]` reads Gerardus's GeoJSON (`GET /api/v1/sites/{l16}/geojson?layers=street_use&frame=site`), keeps the `travel_lane` features, and places them in the sensor frame with the accepted pose: from the pose row once `PUT /api/lidar/sites/{l16}/pose` lands ([visualiser alignment plan](lidar-visualiser-gerardus-alignment-plan.md)), until then from a registration record given on the command line as protojson. It uses HTTP and JSON, so no Gerardus proto is generated into Go (ADR-0006 decision 4). The web Sites page shows the copy's date and a Pull button for an operator on the tailnet.
- **Radar-only sites** have lanes too: the painter draws them by hand over the city streets with their limits, no scan needed, and the pull is the same.

### 2. Directions

A **direction** is a named group of lanes: the lanes at a site sharing a `street` whose bearings lie within 45° of their circular mean. Its id is a slug of the street and the compass word of the mean bearing (`columbus-ave-nb`), its label `Columbus Ave northbound`, its bearing the mean, its limit the lanes' `maxspeed` when they agree and a per-lane list when they differ. Computed on pull and stored on `site_lanes.direction_id`, so the names are stable between pulls unless a lane's street or bearing changes. A site with two streets has up to four directions; the survey's **primary street**, named on the deployment or the site, decides which two the survey page leads with (§6).

**Fallback without lanes.** Until a site has approved lanes, directions come from the tracks: each car track's direction is the circular mean of its headings at instants moving at 2 m/s or more; the two largest opposed modes of those directions, when each holds at least 20% of tracks, are two directions named by compass word only (`northbound`, `southbound`), with `source: "heading"` and no limit; tracks more than 60° off both are `other` (turning, or a cross street) and are counted, not binned. This gives every site two histograms now, and the lanes refine the names and supply the limits later.

### 3. Attribution (L8)

A new neutral package, `internal/lidar/lanegeom`, imports no layer and holds what the [lane stitching plan](lidar-lane-stitching-plan.md#the-lane-map) asked for in the same words: a directed segment with a corridor half-width, projection of a point to (segment, arc length, lateral offset, heading error), membership with hysteresis, and route distance through successors. A `site_lanes` row is one segment with half-width `width_m / 2 + 0.5 m`. The L8 `PathFrame` interface is satisfied by a segment, so following can read an approved lane where it fits one today.

`l8analytics.AttributeLanes(observations, lanes)` assigns each observed instant the lanes whose corridor holds it with heading within 45° of the lane's direction, and each **track** the lane holding at least 60% of its moving instants, with its direction; otherwise `unassigned` with a reason: `no_lane`, `ambiguous` (two lanes, neither over 60%), `wrong_way` (inside lanes, heading against every one). A track whose instants fall in two lanes **end to end** (a limit change at a sign) contributes its maximum speed within each lane to that lane's population separately, as the posted-limits plan requires, and its site-wide maximum once to the site's population as today, so the headline numbers do not change.

Attribution is computed when a report, chart or export asks, from the stored observations and the lanes copy, and the output names the `fetched_at` of the copy and the attribution method version. Nothing is written per track.

### 4. Reports and the charts API

- **`data.json`** gains `directions[]`: id, label, bearing, `limit` {value, unit, source, jurisdiction} or null, lane ids, `n`, p50, p85, p98, max, the 5 mph histogram, the share at or above the limit and by 5, 10 and 15 mph bands (the speeding-attitudes item), `unassigned` counts by reason, and the lanes copy's `fetched_at`. The site-wide block is unchanged (D-18).
- **PDF**: a "By direction" page: the two primary directions' histograms side by side on one speed axis and one share ceiling, each with its own LIMIT line and label; a table of the key numbers per direction with its limit and source; the cross-street directions, when present, in the table only. Masked under 50 transits per direction as the temporal strata will be, with the mask stated.
- **Fallback**: a direction whose lanes carry no `maxspeed` uses `ReportConfig.SpeedLimit` and says so ("operator-supplied, applied to this direction"), as the posted-limits plan already requires of the per-request limit.
- **Charts API**: `/api/charts/histogram` and `/api/charts/comparison` take `direction=<id>`; `/api/sites/{id}/directions` lists a site's directions with their limits.

### 5. Radar

Phase C first confirms, at ingest, whether the OPS243's sign reaches `radar_data.speed` or is lost to a magnitude or a device-side filter; the finding goes in this plan. Then:

- the sign is kept on `radar_data.speed`, and a generated `direction` column (+1 approaching, −1 receding) is added there and as `transit_direction` on `radar_data_transits`, filled by the transit worker from the sign of its readings;
- approaching and receding map to street directions through the deployment's **sensor heading** (V32 in the vocabulary plan: the beam centre's bearing, clockwise from north): approaching traffic travels at the heading plus 180°, receding at the heading; each takes the direction of §2 whose bearing lies within 45° of it, and so that direction's limit;
- if the radar's footprint on the road spans a limit change in a direction (two lanes end to end within it), compliance for that direction is suppressed with that reason and the histogram is still shown, as the posted-limits plan says;
- the radar rollups and `summary.json` (§6) carry `directions[]` in the same shape as the LiDAR's.

### 6. The scene export and the survey site

**Export.** `vehicle_speed` in `timeline.json` gains `directions[]` in the shape of §4, computed by the timeline accumulator from the exported tracks' velocities: from the lanes copy when the site has one (the export reads the copy and names its `fetched_at` in the header), else the heading fallback of §2. `summary.json` for a radar survey carries the same `directions[]` with ids from the deployment heading. The export test that refuses personal fields and trajectories (D-34) extends to the new block: counts and bins only.

**Model** (`survey-model.js`). `summariseSpeed` normalises `directions` beside the whole: per direction the same `bins`, `count`, `p50`, `p85`, `p98`, `max`, plus `label`, `bearing`, `limit` (the direction's own, else the site's `speed_limit_mph` as the fallback it already is) and `limitSource`. `shareAtOrAbove`, `excessBands` and `speedFinding` take a direction; `speedFinding` grows a two-direction sentence ("Northbound, 12.4% reached 25 mph or more. Southbound is signed 30 mph; 3.1% reached it.") as segments, so the static page and the browser render the same words.

**Charts** (`survey-charts.js`). A new `directionPairSvg(directions, opts)`: two histograms side by side on **one speed axis (in the display unit) and one share ceiling**, shared across both directions and across the recordings being compared (`speedScale` and `shareCeiling` take every direction's bins), each panel with its own LIMIT line and label, its P50, P85 and P98 ticks, and its compass word, street and bearing arrow in the title. On narrow screens the panels stack. The mirrored "butterfly" form (two histograms back to back on a shared vertical axis) was considered and set aside: it reads well when the question is symmetry, and badly when the two limit lines sit at different speeds, which is the case this plan exists for; it can return as a toggle. `sparklineSvg` gains a two-row variant for the listing.

**Templates.** [family-speed.njk](../../public_html/src/_includes/survey/family-speed.njk) shows the pair when a recording has two directions, with the whole-site histogram moved into "Full distribution"; the key numbers become one row per direction, each with its limit and a `LAW` tag only where the limit is a posted one; the "At or above the limit" bands are per direction. The listing's "Speed distribution" column shows the two-row sparkline and "P85 / limit" shows both directions. Compare mode compares per direction: `percentileRowsSvg` groups rows by direction, and the compare histograms are pairs on a shared scale. The data-and-methods section says how directions were assigned (lanes approved in Gerardus on a date, or headings) and names the lanes copy's date.

**Site data.** `speed_limit_mph` on the field mark stays the site fallback. A new optional `speed_limits_by_direction` on the mark (`{"nb": {"mph": 25, "source": "sign"}, "sb": {"mph": 30, "source": "sign"}}`) lets the owner record the two signs now for the heading fallback, and `make render-scene-data` prefers the lanes' limits once a site's export carries them. `scenes.json` and `check-scene-data` carry it through.

**Tests.** Jest in `public_html/src/js/__tests__`: the model's per-direction numbers equal the whole's when one direction holds every track; the pair SVG's two panels share an axis and ceiling and carry two limit lines; the finding's wording for equal and for different limits; the listing sort by P85 uses the primary direction. Snapshots for the pair at the wide and the narrow size.

## Open questions

- Which street is a survey's primary street at a two-street site: the deployment names it, or the direction with the most tracks? The deployment, with the busiest pair as the default until set.
- Should a direction's histogram be k-thresholded per bin on the public site, as Gerardus's aggregates are? The site publishes whole-site bins with small counts today; this plan keeps that and prints n. The owner may tighten it.
- Do the temporal strata of v0.7.7 cross with directions in the PDF (four tables) or stay separate? Separate until a reader asks.
- Does the heading fallback belong in the export forever as the method of record for sites without lanes, or is it retired when the last site has lanes? Kept, labelled `source: heading`.

## Not in scope

- The lane field, proposals and their review: the [lanes from tracks plan](lidar-lane-proposals-from-tracks-plan.md) and Gerardus.
- Schedules (D-16), effective dates and signs read from the scans: the posted-limits plan's items, which attach to a lane by its id when they land.
- Per-lane speed reporting on the public site: lanes feed directions there; per-lane tables are a report feature.
- Any per-track output: a direction is counts and bins.
