# Human following behind autonomous vehicles

**Evidence:** Mironenko et al., _European Transport Research Review_ (October 2026), described in the briefing as a synthesis of 37 studies and 49 evidence cases. Verify study coverage and coding against the full review. Related primary study: _Automated vehicles drive safer but induce riskier behavior in human drivers in car-following scenarios_, _Communications Engineering_ (2026), https://doi.org/10.1038/s44172-026-00793-3.

**Observable hypothesis:** under comparable conditions, do human followers exhibit different THW distributions when the leader is a confidently classified Waymo/Zoox fleet vehicle rather than a conventional vehicle? Distinguish **vehicle fleet/model identification** from **verified automated-driving engagement**. LiDAR alone cannot establish human motive, trust, or strategic exploitation.

**Study design:** same-site/lane/time comparisons, predeclared episode-level estimand, site-specific effects and hierarchical model with site heterogeneity. Consider leader speed and acceleration variability, traffic density, road enclosure, geometry, occlusion and matching/mediator bias. Use pilot variance to simulate power; 3,000 episodes is not an established requirement.

**Immediate blocker:** the [October 20 opportunity report](../../lidar/operations/following-opportunity-corpus-2026-10.md) reports median track-pair duration 0.5 s, centroid-based gap and substantial identity fragmentation. These are **not yet** validated independent following episodes. First validate episode continuity, physical bumper gaps, vehicle classification and selection denominators.

**Outcome:** preregister a noncausal observational comparison with equivalence analysis and cross-site sensitivity; do not publish an AV safety or aggression score.
